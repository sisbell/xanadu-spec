# ASN-0134: Substrate Consistency and Isolation Model

*2026-06-13*

*What the sequential substrate promises a concurrent client: the step is the atom, the home is the unit of contention, and the snapshot is the only honest referent for a verdict.*

**Depends:** ASN-0047 (Transition Model — for the account's document sub-allocator `A_doc`, §4), ASN-0086 (Typed Relations on Address Sets), ASN-0093 (Allocation Substrate), ASN-0126 (Substrate Shape Framework), ASN-0128 (Substrate Type Operational Semantics).

We are handed a substrate whose entire dynamics is a *sequence*. The governing axiom — `SequentialTransitionAxiom`, asserted in ASN-0093 and inherited verbatim by ASN-0128's gated relation `→_sh` — says that transitions `Σ → Σ'` are atomic, uninterruptible, and totally ordered, and that `Σ →* Σ'` is the reflexive-transitive closure of single steps. The operation surface of ASN-0128 is defined the same way: `Emit_K`, `Nullify_Binary`, `Observe_K` are specified by what one step does to one state. And yet every layer raised on this substrate presumes a crowd. A predicate may be evaluated by an *arbitrary* observer. Quiescence — "is everyone done?" — must be recognizable *while writers may still be active*. A definition's content must be allocated as one contiguous run *even as other agents allocate*. So the question is forced upon us: a definition written in the singular is being *used* in the plural, and we must say exactly what survives the translation.

This note does not add a concurrency-control mechanism — no lock, no transaction manager, no scheduler. Nelson never specified one (his own consultations are candid that "no locking protocol... no concurrent-editing semantics" appears in *Literary Machines*, and that the back end was left to supply it), and Gregory's `udanax-green` supplies exactly one realization (a single-threaded event loop) rather than a contract. What we owe is the *contract* that sits between them: the weakest discipline an implementation must honour so that the sequential semantics is faithfully presented to concurrent clients. We will find that the contract is much weaker than the global serialization Gregory's loop happens to provide — it is *per-home*, not global — and that this is not an accident of engineering but precisely Nelson's design intent, where "order is always local, fixed at one home, by order of arrival," never "a synchronous agreement reached among all observers." This localization is a *writer-side allocation* result; §8 and §9 isolate where per-home order does not suffice.

## 1. The grain: a step, not a batch

We begin by fixing what "an execution" is, because every later claim is a statement about it. By `SequentialTransitionAxiom` the substrate's behaviour under any number of agents is a single sequence of states

`𝔼 :  Σ₀ → Σ₁ → Σ₂ → ⋯`

in which each `Σ_i → Σ_{i+1}` is one atomic step `σ_i` drawn from the step vocabulary of ASN-0128's gated relation, `K = {K.σ, K.α, K.λ_sh}` — that is `→_sh ≡ K.σ ∪ K.α ∪ K.λ_sh`: document registration, content allocation, and gated link/tuple emission. We anchor the sequence at its initial state, `Σ₀ = Σ_init` — ASN-0126/0128's registry-adjoined initial state, `→_sh*`-reachable from itself — so that, every `σ_i` being a `→_sh` step, each `Σ_k` on `𝔼` is `→_sh*`-reachable from `Σ_init` via the prefix `Σ₀ → ⋯ → Σ_k`. (We commit `𝔼` to this one substrate stack — ASN-0093's allocation model carried up through ASN-0086/0126/0128.) Write `idx(σ) = i` for a step's position. We index states by their position in this one order; `Σ_i →* Σ_j` holds exactly when `i ≤ j`. This sequence is not a description of how an implementation is built — Gregory's loop builds it one way, a future per-home scheduler might build it another — it is the *meaning* the implementation must present. What we reason about is `𝔼` and the states on it; the scheduler that produces `𝔼` is out of scope (see *What this note does not cover*).

The first thing to extract is the observational reading of atomicity. The axiom says steps are *uninterruptible*; observationally this means:

> **A0 (StepAtomicity).** There is no state strictly between `Σ_i` and `Σ_{i+1}`. The states an observer can ever name are exactly `{Σ_k : k ∈ 𝔼}`.

A0 is not a new assumption; it is `SequentialTransitionAxiom` read as a constraint on what can be seen: a half-applied step is not a state of `𝔼`, so it is not nameable, so it cannot be read.

Now we connect operations to steps. An agent *proposes* an operation; the substrate *realizes* it as activity on `𝔼`. We must be precise about the realization count, because it is where "the unit of atomicity" is decided.

> **A1 (Realization).** A *single* state-changing operation — one not issued as a batch — is realized as *exactly one* atomic step, at a unique index; distinct such operations occupy distinct indices. (A multi-step batch is many steps, not one operation — A5.) An operation that leaves the state unchanged is realized as *zero* steps — it reads and returns, committing no step — and this happens in four ways: a *read-only query* — an `Observe_K`, or any of ASN-0128's behavioral *reads* (D1–D4; BH1–BH3; BH4's `age`/`stale` — but not BH4's `retract_stale`, a multi-step batch by A5), each committing no `→_sh` step; an idempotent `Emit_K` hit; the hit branch of a `Nullify_Binary`; and a *rejected* call, one whose precondition fails at the state it is evaluated against — e.g., a gate, `P0`, or `P-tgt` failure (ASN-0128 S3, I6). The first three return a value read off the state; the fourth returns failure and adjoins nothing. These four are the *single-operation* zero-step cases; an empty `m = 0` fire (a `retract_stale` over an empty stale set, A5) is the fifth, degenerate-batch zero-step case.
>
> Whether a *would-be* state-changing operation realizes as one step or is *rejected to zero* can itself depend on the state it meets, hence on the linearization: a `Nullify_Binary` whose `P-tgt` holds at one index and fails at another fires in the first case and is rejected in the second. An operation's very presence in the realized step set can be order-dependent.

A1's "exactly one / exactly zero" is read straight off ASN-0128: an `Emit_K` *miss* invokes `K.λ_sh` (one step), while a hit, an `Observe_K`, any behavioral read, and a *rejected* call all "take no →_sh step" (ASN-0128 I1, S3, I6) — zero steps — and distinct steps occupy distinct indices by the totality of the order. We have therefore answered the first half of the headline question already, and can now name the linearization point:

> **A2 (LinearizationPoint).** A state-changing operation `op` realized as step `σ` has linearization point `lin(op) = idx(σ)`; its *entire* effect is the single transition `Σ_{lin(op)} → Σ_{lin(op)+1}`. A zero-step operation realized as a *single bounded access* has `lin(op)` equal to the one index it reads; its effect on state is the identity. A zero-step operation realized as *several* bounded accesses — a `stale` enumeration or a cross-type join, which read multiple committed states at distinct indices (§8) — has no single read index and hence no single `lin`; its soundness is the province of V0/V2 (§8), not of any one linearization point. Its effect on state is likewise the identity. (So A1 classes both kinds as zero-step; A2 hands a single `lin` only to the single-access kind; §8 governs the rest.)

The phrase "its entire effect is the single transition" is the operative content. A typed emission does not dribble its effect across several states; it is in `Σ_{lin}` not at all and in `Σ_{lin+1}` in full. This is what makes the *typed emission* coincide with the *step* as the atomic unit. The third candidate the question offers — "a whole fire/batch" — is a different animal, and we now show it is *not* atomic once it spans two or more steps.

A *batch* (we use the words *fire* and *batch* interchangeably) is a finite collection of operations issued as one logical act by one agent or one rule firing — a `retract_stale` issuing one `Nullify_Binary` per stale event (ASN-0128 BH4, which states outright it is "a sequence of wrapper steps, not an atomic operation"), or a definition's content run, one `K.α` per atom of the definition. Its constituent steps land at some indices `i₁ < i₂ < ⋯ < i_m` in `𝔼`. Call the batch **contiguous** when `{i₁,…,i_m} = {i₁, i₁+1, …, i₁+m−1}`, i.e. no foreign step interleaves; otherwise **split**.

> **A5 (NoBatchIsolation).** A multi-step batch (`m ≥ 2`) is not atomic, in two independent senses. *(Partial visibility — always, modulo the read surface.)* For *any* batch, contiguous or split, the interior states *exist*, and each carries a strict, non-empty *prefix* of the batch's own effects: the batch's first atom is committed at `Σ_{i₁+1}` while its last is not committed until `Σ_{i_m+1}`, and a reader may land at any interior index `k` with `i₁ < k ≤ i_m` (A0, A3). Whether that prefix is *read-witnessed* turns on the batch's subspace, because §8 fixes the read surface as link-store reads (active views `Observe_K`, link-subspace frontier descents) with no read over content population: a *link-store* batch — a `retract_stale` issuing its `Nullify_Binary`s — has each partial effect visible to an `Observe_K` over the active slice `A_K`, so an interior read returns some events retracted and others not; a *content* run (one `K.α` per atom) is non-atomic only *structurally* — its mid-batch state exists and is canonical (A6), but its prefix is witnessed by no read the note models. This needs no foreign step — it follows from `m ≥ 2` alone. *(Foreign interleaving — when split.)* If the batch is *additionally* split, some foreign step at index `j` with `i₁ ≤ j < i_m` interleaves, so an interior `Σ_{j+1}` also carries effects *not* belonging to the batch. Either way the batch's partial effects are observable; the substrate's all-or-nothing guarantee stops at the single step (A0). The *degenerate* batch sizes are atomic after all — an `m = 1` fire is a single step, indivisible by A0 exactly as a lone operation is, and an `m = 0` fire (an empty `retract_stale`) commits no step, so there is no interior index to land on and the partial-visibility argument has no foothold. Non-atomicity is a strictly `m ≥ 2` phenomenon.

The contrast with A2 is the whole point. A single step is indivisible by A0; a batch is divisible, and we must keep its two modes of divisibility apart, because a layer that wants a batch to *appear atomic* must defeat both and they are not the same thing. *Contiguity* — no foreign step wedged between the batch's first and last step — is a *writer-side* critical section (W2, §6); it defeats the second mode (foreign interleaving) and nothing else. *Atomicity* — no reader ever observes a partial batch — is strictly stronger: it must additionally keep a reader out of the batch's *interior states*, and a `(d, s_C)` critical section, which excludes only writers, does nothing toward that.

## 2. What an observation witnesses

We can now say exactly what `Observe_K`, evaluated by any agent at any time, sees relative to an `Emit_K` or `Nullify_Binary` in flight. The answer is fixed by A0, A1, and the single-state reading A3 isolates, and it is clean.

> **A3 (SnapshotRead).** Two readings must be held apart. *Abstractly*, `Observe_K(Σ)` is — by its ASN-0086 definition as a function of `Σ` — a total function of the single state `Σ`, a property of the definition that holds irrespective of concurrency. *Realized*, the `Observe` a concurrent implementation performs is a *bounded access* whose internal sub-reads — for `Observe_K`, the `A_K = L_K ∖ nullified` computation over the link store and the retraction slice — each land on some committed state of `𝔼`. That they land on *one* committed state `Σ_k`, so that the realized read returns `Observe_K(Σ_k)` — a total function of the single `Σ_k`, depending on no other state — is *not* delivered by the read's zero-step character (which secures only that it advances `𝔼` by no step of its own, A1); it is exactly MIC clause 4's obligation, the single-state realization of a bounded access. Absent clause 4 the sub-reads may straddle a commit and compound two committed states into one read.

The realized register is what the downstream guarantees build on. From A3's realized reading — single-state under clause 4 — and A0 we get the read-isolation theorem:

> **A4 (NoTornStep).** No `Observe` witnesses a partial step. For an `Observe` at index `k` and a state-changing `op` with `lin(op) = i`: if `k ≤ i` the `Observe` sees none of `op`'s effect; if `k ≥ i+1` it sees all of it. There is no third case.
>
> *Proof.* `op`'s effect is precisely the transition `Σ_i → Σ_{i+1}` (A2). The realized `Observe` reads a single committed state `Σ_k` — the index clause 4 pins it to (A3's realized register) — which is either at-or-before `Σ_i` (`k ≤ i`, effect absent) or at-or-after `Σ_{i+1}` (`k ≥ i+1`, effect present). The excluded middle — a state strictly between `Σ_i` and `Σ_{i+1}` — does not exist (A0). ∎

So the disjunction in the headline question — "only committed state, or can it witness a partial effect?" — resolves to the first horn for any single emission or nullification: **only committed state**. An `Emit_K` "in flight" is, from the snapshot's vantage, either not-yet-linearized (its step has not happened; the reader sees the pre-state) or linearized (its step has happened; the reader sees the full post-effect). "In flight" never means "half-applied," because there is no half-state to be in. This is the formal core of Nelson's intent that "a torn read is impossible": content lives at permanent addresses and is never rewritten in place, so the only thing a step changes is which addresses are *present* in a store (and, for a link tuple, which it relates) — and that change is the step itself, indivisible.

The one honest qualification is A5's: while no *step* is ever torn, a *multi-step batch* is not protected. A reader can land between the constituent nullifications of a `retract_stale` and see some events retracted and others not; it can land partway through a definition's content run and see only a prefix of its atoms. We must therefore characterize *what kind of state* a mid-batch snapshot is, so that a reader who lands there is not misled about its validity.

The `→_sh` stack gives a sharp answer — sharper, in fact, than the read-isolation story usually told of a transactional store. Every invariant the stack carries is *step-local* — either a per-state predicate that every reachable state satisfies on its own, or a transition invariant that every single `→_sh` step preserves — with no class of properties reserved for the boundaries of multi-step composites. That step-locality is established in A6, on one reachability argument rather than a curated roster. The argumentative content here is what that step-locality *denies*: there is, in this stack, no provenance relation to leave dangling and no arrangement to leave half-coupled — no boundary-reserved property class, and hence no coupling obligation that a *later* step must discharge.

> **A6 (CanonicalState).** Call a state *structurally canonical* iff every per-state invariant of the `→_sh` stack — every invariant that is a predicate of a single state — holds at it. *Every* state on `𝔼` is `→_sh*`-reachable — the prefix `Σ₀ → ⋯ → Σ_k` witnesses `Σ_init →_sh* Σ_k` (§1) — and reachability is exactly the hypothesis the foundations' transfer lemmas require, so `B2`/`RP-a` carry *every* per-state stack invariant to `𝔼`'s states. Hence *every* `Σ_k` on `𝔼` is structurally canonical. The one external constant any such invariant reads against is registry-fixity's `R₀ := Σ_init.registry`, the construction-fixed initial record (ASN-0128 `R-VAL`), single-state content of ASN-0126's `P1` / ASN-0128's `R1`. Canonicity is genuinely single-state: each conjunct is a predicate of `Σ_k`'s own components, checkable against `Σ_k` and the external constant `R₀` alone, never against another state.
>
> The stack's *transition* invariants — append-only monotonicity and value-immutability `C0` (content) and `L12` (link), and ASN-0093's document-set monotonicity `M1` (`(A Σ → Σ' :: dom(M) ⊆ dom(M'))`, no step removes a registered document) — have the form `(A Σ → Σ' :: …)` with no single-state shadow, so a companion *transition clause* carries them on the same reachability base: for every `k ≥ 1`, the step `Σ_{k-1} → Σ_k` on `𝔼` preserves `C0`, `L12`, and `M1`, by `B2`'s transition clause and `RP-b`. The chain-contiguity members — `ChainMembershipForOrigin`, with `L-ContiguousPrefix` its link-store form — hold at every state of `𝔼` by reachability. Their *preservation* under any interleaving is the earliest model-intrinsic result: the allocator deposits only at the next sibling of a home's current chain-maximum (`inc(max,·)`; ASN-0093 `FirstEmission`/`SubsequentEmissionFreshness`), no step ever removes a slot (`C0`/`L12`), and a step into any other home or subspace leaves this home's population untouched — so a gapless initial segment stays a gapless initial segment step by step, under *any* interleaving and with no appeal to per-home order.

But A6 is a statement about *structure*, and it is silent about *settledness* — and the two must not be run together. A reader who lands mid-batch sees a state that is *completely* canonical: addresses partitioned correctly, every stored tuple shape-conformant and registered, every chain gapless and conformant. Nothing in it is marked "mid-batch." A `retract_stale` that has nullified three of its five targets leaves a state indistinguishable, *by every invariant the stack carries*, from a settled state in which only those three were ever to be retracted; a content run halted after two of its atoms is indistinguishable from a settled two-atom definition. So there is no "precise and only sense of incomplete" that a snapshot exposes — incompleteness is not a property of the state at all, but a *relation* between the state and a batch that happens to span it, and that relation is invisible from inside the snapshot. This gap — canonical but possibly mid-batch — is the substrate's actual read-isolation guarantee, the one Nelson left "correctly open" at the multi-step level while the single-step level was closed by construction.

## 3. Linearization: how independent proposals become one order

The question asks whether there is a linearization point and how independently-proposed operations become positions in the one order. A2 already named the point. What remains is to say what an implementation must do to *honour* it, since the abstract model simply postulates the order while an implementation must produce it from genuinely concurrent proposals.

First the client model, since the consistency level we may claim turns on it. An agent may issue operations *concurrently* — *pipelining*: holding several in flight at once, issuing the next before the prior's acknowledgment returns — so the substrate models no per-agent *program order* over an agent's issues and preserves none. §4's cross-home liberation places a pipelining agent's two distinct-home operations at `≺`-incomparable indices the linearization may resolve either way. What consistency level this client model permits — *serializability*, but pointedly not sequential consistency, the order *logical, not temporal* — G0 states and proves.

> **G0 (Serializability, under a pipelined client model).** *Client model (the pipelining client above):* per-agent *program order* is neither modeled nor preserved. The substrate realizes a single total order of atomic steps (`SequentialTransitionAxiom`), each operation appearing atomic at its `lin`; so every execution is *serializable* — equivalent to the serial order `𝔼`. The order is logical (a per-home arrival sequencing), carrying no global temporal coordinate that observers must agree on — Nelson is emphatic that "time is not included in the tumbler; time is kept track of separately," so the address space refuses to encode the global timeline a stronger, *linearizable* guarantee would demand. We name this *serializability*, and pointedly *not* *sequential consistency* — but the conclusion takes two steps that must be kept apart, because the first does not reach it alone. *(1) The substrate does not enforce program order.* An agent's operations into *distinct* homes are `≺`-incomparable (§4), and the linearization resolves them without regard to which the agent issued first. This by itself is *no* SC violation: distinct-home operations commute (H1, G1(ii)), so reordering them is observationally benign — the program-order serial order reaches the very same state, and *that* execution is sequentially consistent. Breaking SC needs an execution whose observations *no* program-order-respecting serial order can reproduce. *(2) A third-party observer exhibits one.* Let agent P emit, in program order, `A` into home `α` then `B` into home `β` (`α ≠ β`); pipelining leaves the two `≺`-incomparable, so the linearization may commit `B` before `A` (H1: both orders are valid and reach the same state), opening a real window in which `B` is present and `A` is absent. Let observer Q, in its own program order, `Observe` `β` then `α` at two indices inside that window: Q sees `B` *present*, then `A` *absent*. Any SC serial order `S` must respect both program orders — `A <_S B` for P, `(read β) <_S (read α)` for Q — and reproduce Q's reads — `B <_S (read β)` (B seen present) and `(read α) <_S A` (A seen absent). Together these force the cycle `A <_S B <_S (read β) <_S (read α) <_S A`, so no such `S` exists: the execution is *not* sequentially consistent. This coexists with the *linearizability* §3 grants under A7 — but the coexistence is *not* a breach of the textbook theorem Lin⟹SC, and seeing why forces us to be explicit that the note's "linearizability" is **real-time-precedence-only**, hence *strictly weaker* than the textbook notion. Textbook linearizability asks its witnessing serial order to do two things: respect real-time precedence between non-overlapping operations, *and* — through its equivalence clause, that the order reproduce each process's own subhistory — respect each process's *program* order. That second, program-order conjunct is exactly what lets textbook linearizability imply SC. We keep only the first: A7 pins a linearization point that orders a response before any strictly-later invocation, and constrains nothing about one agent's internally-overlapping issues. So the note's notion does *not* imply SC — and no theorem is contradicted, because Lin⟹SC assumes *sequential-process* clients, whose program order *is* their real-time order (each operation issued only after the prior's response, so no two overlap); under that hypothesis the real-time conjunct already delivers the program-order conjunct, and SC follows. A *pipelining* client withdraws the hypothesis: P issues B before A's response returns, so A and B overlap and program order ⊄ real-time order on that pair. This is the seam, and it forces a reconciliation we state plainly, because the two halves of G0 read P's pair *differently, and must*: the SC witness treats A,B as *program-ordered* — it must, to force `A <_S B` and close its cycle — while the linearizability claim treats the *same* pair as *unordered*, since they overlap in real time and real-time-only linearizability fixes no serial order between overlapping operations. Pipelining is precisely where program order and real-time order diverge, and that divergence — not any failure of Lin⟹SC, whose sequential-process premise pipelining denies — is what carries "real-time-linearizable ∧ ¬SC." A *sequential* client recovers SC by its own acknowledgment discipline (issuing each operation after the prior's response), not by any substrate promise. The substrate that serves both client styles guarantees only serializability.

An implementation strengthens G0 to *linearizability* — in the real-time-precedence-only sense just delimited (the order respecting real-time precedence between a response and a subsequent invocation), *not* the SC-entailing textbook notion — exactly when it commits the effect before it acknowledges the operation. Gregory's evidence pins the realized linearization point precisely: the in-memory enfilade *is* the shared canonical state, and a write becomes visible to every other session at the completion of the `xanadu(&task)` dispatch — "the mutation is applied at mutation-function-return time, but it is not reachable by any other session until the cooperative event loop returns." That is a clean linearization point at commit. But Gregory also surfaces a place where the realization *under*-honours the contract: for `INSERT`, `DELETEVSPAN`, and `REARRANGE`, the response is emitted *before* the mutation executes ("response-before-check"), so a client can receive "success" for an effect not yet committed (and which may silently fail). An implementation honouring the contract must not do this. We therefore record acknowledgment ordering as a contract clause, not an optional nicety:

> **A7 (CommitBeforeAcknowledge).** For the linearization point of A2 to be presentable to a concurrent client, an operation's response/return value must be produced only at or after `lin(op)`. An acknowledgment emitted before the committing step is unsound: it lets a caller act on an effect that is not yet (and may never be) in the order.

With A7, a reader or caller never observes — never *acts upon* — an uncommitted effect, which is the linearizability strengthening of A4 from "reads" to "the responses agents build their next move on."

How, then, do two independently-proposed operations *become* positions? They are *placed* — by whatever scheduler the implementation runs — into distinct indices (A1). The placement mechanism is out of scope; its *existence and injectivity* is `SequentialTransitionAxiom`. The interesting question is not *that* a placement exists but *how much* the placement is allowed to vary without disturbing the semantics — because that is what tells an implementer how much concurrency they may safely permit.

## 4. Where the order must live: per-home, not global

Before the conflict analysis, one scoping commitment, and the place of document registration within it. Throughout this section and the contract of §9 an *allocation step* is a `K.α` or a `K.λ_sh` — an emission into one of a *document's* content or link sub-allocators `A_{s_C}(d)`, `A_{s_L}(d)` — and the theory below is a theory of those frontiers. Document registration `K.σ` remains a step of `𝔼`, to which the step-level claims A0–A7 (canonicity A6 included) apply unchanged. Its *frontier* status, by contrast, is not handed to us by the committed stack: the carry-over of H0/H1/H2 to `K.σ` — and the account-tier obligation it places on §9's clause 2 — must be stated *conditionally on the realization* rather than asserted of `𝔼`.

Consider what an allocating step actually reads and writes. By ASN-0093 and the `FrontierUnification` lemma of ASN-0126, an emission into a home `d`'s sub-allocator for subspace `S ∈ {s_C, s_L}` reads the home's current *frontier* and deposits at it. Let us make the frontier uniform across both subspaces. For `S ∈ {s_C, s_L}` write `dom_S(Σ)` for `dom(Σ.C)` when `S = s_C` and `dom(Σ.L)` when `S = s_L`, and define the home's `S`-population and `S`-frontier index

`P_S(d, Σ) = {a ∈ dom_S(Σ) : origin(a) = d}`,  `φ_S(d, Σ) = |P_S(d, Σ)|`.

By `ChainMembershipForOrigin` (ASN-0093), `P_S(d, Σ)` is a *contiguous initial segment* of the chain `A_S(d)`, so `φ_S(d, Σ)` is the count of already-emitted slots and the next emission lands at chain slot `φ_S(d, Σ)`, advancing the count by one. (For `S = s_L` this is exactly ASN-0126's `a_emit(Σ,d) = chain_d(f_d^Σ)`; for `S = s_C` it is `SubsequentEmissionFreshness`'s `inc(a_prev, 0)` with `a_prev` the home's content maximum. The two are one mechanism.)

> **H0 (FrontierDiscipline).** Along `𝔼`, for fixed `(d, S)`, `φ_S(d, ·)` is non-decreasing, increases by exactly one at each `S`-allocation to `d`, and is left unchanged by every other step.
>
> *Proof.* An `S`-allocation to `d` adjoins one fresh address with `origin = d` and subspace `S` (ASN-0093 K.α / ASN-0128 K.λ_sh effect), so `|P_S(d, ·)|` rises by one. Any other step is either a `K.σ`, which registers a document and frames both stores (`C' = C ∧ L' = L`, ASN-0093 K.σ), leaving every `dom_S` fixed; or an allocation into a different `(d', S') ≠ (d, S)`, which adjoins one fresh address `a'` with `origin(a') = d'` to store `dom_{S'}` and frames everything else. That `a'` falls outside `P_S(d, ·) = {a ∈ dom_S(·) : origin(a) = d}` needs no disjointness lemma — only the set's own two filters, store-membership in `dom_S` and `origin = d`, against the fact that `(d', S') ≠ (d, S)` diverges along at least one axis. *If `S' ≠ S`* (whether or not `d' = d`): the step writes only `dom_{S'}` and leaves `dom_S` framed, so `P_S(d, ·) ⊆ dom_S` is untouched outright. *If `S' = S`* (which forces `d' ≠ d`): the step does add `a'` to `dom_S`, but `origin(a') = d' ≠ d` fails the `origin = d` filter, and — `origin` being a structural projection fixed per address and `dom_S` append-only — no prior member departs either, so `P_S(d, ·)` is again unchanged. These two cases exhaust `(d', S') ≠ (d, S)`; the cross-home, cross-subspace pair (`d' ≠ d ∧ S' ≠ S`) falls under the first. In every case `P_S(d, ·)` is unchanged. ∎

H0 is the lever. It shows that the *only* steps that touch `(d, S)`'s frontier are the `S`-allocations to `d` themselves. From this the conflict structure of allocation falls out immediately.

> **H1 (CrossHomeIndependence).** Two allocation steps into distinct `(d, S) ≠ (d', S')` *commute*: neither reads or writes state the other touches, and applying them in either order yields the same state.
>
> *Proof.* The first reads `φ_S(d,·)` and writes one address on chain `A_S(d)`; the second reads `φ_{S'}(d',·)` and writes on `A_{S'}(d')`. We show the two chains are disjoint as address sets, across the two axes along which `(d, S) ≠ (d', S')` can differ. *If `d ≠ d'`:* we argue by *origin*, because origin settles the cross-document case uniformly across subspaces — including the cross-document, cross-subspace `S ≠ S'` pair that `CrossDocumentDisjointness`'s single-`·` statement leaves unnamed. The address the first step deposits joins `P_S(d,·)`, defined by the `origin = d` filter, so it carries `origin = d`; the second's deposit carries `origin = d'`. Since `origin` is a structural projection fixed per address (H0) — a function of the address alone — `a = a'` would force `d = origin(a) = origin(a') = d'`, contradicting `d ≠ d'`. Hence the two deposits are distinct and `A_S(d) ∩ A_{S'}(d') = ∅` *regardless of subspace*. *If `d = d'`* (which forces `S ≠ S'`): the two chains share an origin, so origin cannot separate them; instead the sibling-subspace anchors `[d.0.s_C]` and `[d.0.s_L]` diverge at the subspace digit, so by T10 (PartitionIndependence) the chains are disjoint — `DisjointSubAllocatorChains`. In every case the deposits are distinct and the chains disjoint. By H0 neither step changes the other's frontier population. So each step's precondition (its frontier value) and effect (its deposited key) are on state disjoint from the other's; they commute. ∎

> **H2 (SameHomeConflict).** Two allocation steps into the *same* `(d, S)` conflict: each reads `φ_S(d,·)`, and the first to commit increments it (H0), so the two *cannot* read the same frontier value without colliding. If both read frontier `φ`, both deposit at chain slot `φ` — the *same address* — violating freshness.
>
> *Proof.* Two emissions into `(d, S)` against a common pre-state `Σ_pre` read the same population `P_S(d, Σ_pre)`, and the emission rule is a function of that population, so they compute the same deposit address — in *either* of its two branches.
>
> *Interior case* (`P_S(d, Σ_pre) ≠ ∅`): `SubsequentEmissionFreshness` gives `a = inc(a_prev, 0)` with `a_prev = max P_S(d, Σ_pre)`; both read the same `a_prev`, hence compute the same `a`.
>
> *First-emission boundary* (`P_S(d, Σ_pre) = ∅`): here `max P_S(d, Σ_pre) = max ∅` is undefined and the subsequent rule does not apply. Instead both emissions satisfy ASN-0093's *first-emission* predicate `{a' : origin(a') = d} = ∅` and both compute the determinate first slot `a = [d.0.S.1]` (`FirstEmission`). This is the race for the *first* allocation into a fresh home, and it collides just as squarely.
>
> In either case the two compute one address; whichever commits first deposits it, and the second's freshness precondition — `a ∉ dom_S`, discharged in the interior case by `SubsequentEmissionFreshness` and at the boundary by `FirstEmissionFreshness` — then fails. The conflict is identical at the boundary and in the interior. ∎

H1 and H2 together are the whole answer to "is ordering per-home-independent or globally serialized." The *conflicts* — the places where order matters — are confined to a single `(home, subspace)`. Everything across homes (and across the two subspaces of one home) commutes. Nelson's account of two simultaneous creators is exactly H1+H2: they are "either in different regions (no shared allocator) — and there is no race to lose — or under the same single allocator, which serializes them." The address space is a tree of owned sub-allocators; two writers contend only when they reach into the *same* sub-allocator, and then a single authority sequences them. Collision is "impossible by construction," with "zero coordination" required across regions.

We can now state the equivalence that liberates an implementation from global serialization. To state it *non-vacuously* we must first name an execution model weaker than the total order `𝔼` — for a total order *already* serializes every pair of steps, cross-home included, so against `𝔼` alone "per-home serialization suffices" would collapse into the empty tautology "the total order is total." The content we are after lives one level below `𝔼`, in the *schedule* an implementation commits to before any particular interleaving is realized.

> **G-PO (PartialScheduleModel).** A *schedule* over a reachable start state `Σ` is a finite set `O` of allocation steps (`K.α`, `K.λ_sh`) into homes already registered at `Σ` — no `K.σ` is scheduled *in this base model*, the register-before-allocate dependency discharged in advance per this section's scoping; `H3` below lifts the result to schedules that do register documents — equipped with a partial order `≺` — the order the implementation *forces* — such that for every `(d, S)` the `S`-allocations to `d` in `O` are pairwise `≺`-comparable (*per-home serialization*), while steps into distinct `(d, S) ≠ (d', S')` need not be. A *linearization* of `(O, ≺)` is any total order extending `≺`; run from `Σ`, each linearization is one execution `𝔼`. The total order of §1 is the special case in which `≺` is itself total.

G-PO admits exactly the schedules an implementer cares about: those that force an order *only within each home* and leave cross-home steps `≺`-incomparable. Against this model the liberation is a genuine claim, not a tautology.

> **G1 (PerHomeSerializabilitySuffices).** Let `(O, ≺)` be any per-home-serialized schedule over a reachable `Σ`. Then (i) *every* linearization of `(O, ≺)` is a valid execution preserving every per-state invariant at every state and every allocation invariant in its final state; and (ii) all linearizations are *confluent* — they reach one and the same final state, with the same address at every chain slot of every home. The cross-home order is free to vary, and no invariant and no committed address depends on how a linearization resolves it.
>
> *Proof.* (i) We secure *validity* first, and read the per-state package off the reachability it yields — for A6 (reachable ⟹ canonical) cannot license per-state invariants until the linearization's steps are known to be valid `→_sh` steps. The one precondition a reordering can disturb is an allocating step's *frontier-freshness*; every other precondition is reordering-*invariant*. Frontier-freshness is a per-home matter: per-home comparability totally orders `(d, S)`'s allocations, so by H0 each reads the frontier its predecessor left and lands at the next slot — its deposit fresh and collision-free — and by H1 no cross-`(d, S)` step disturbs that frontier wherever the linearization places it. The reordering-invariant remainder holds at every position regardless of order: a `K.λ_sh` gate reads only data no transposition moves — `Sh-conf(K, F, G)` the presented value `(F, G)` and the registry-fixed `shape(K)` (ASN-0126 `P4`, ShConfStateIndependence), arity-3 the value alone, and K's registration the registry keys — all immutable across runtime steps (W4 / ASN-0128 `R1`); and every allocation's home precondition `d ∈ dom(M)` survives once met, since `G-PO` schedules only into homes already registered at `Σ` and ASN-0093's `M1` (document-set monotonicity, named in A6's transition clause) admits no document removal, so `d` stays in `dom(M)` at every position. Each step's precondition is thus met at its position, so the linearization is a valid execution — whence each of its states is `→_sh*`-reachable from `Σ`, the prefix reaching it its witness (§1). *Now* — reachability in hand — A6 applies, scoped exactly as §5 requires, carrying every conjunct of its per-state package (the chain-contiguity members included) to every state of every linearization. The final-state allocation invariant G1(i) *separately* promises — same-home uniqueness (H2) — is the one thing per-home comparability buys: two same-`(d, S)` allocations both land fresh rather than lose one to a collision. The split this rests on — contiguity model-intrinsic and free, same-home uniqueness serialization-borne (H2) — is the one A6 established. (ii) Any two linearizations of a finite partial order differ by a finite sequence of transpositions of adjacent `≺`-incomparable steps; incomparable steps here are into distinct `(d, S)` (per-home comparability makes same-`(d, S)` steps comparable), so each transposed pair commutes (H1), leaving the surrounding states and every committed address unchanged. Confluence to a single final state follows, and ASN-0093's sequential results, holding along any one linearization, thereby describe them all. ∎

G1 as proved governs an allocation-only schedule, but a real execution also *registers* documents — `𝔼` carries `K.σ` steps. We now make good on §4's deferral and place `K.σ` within the per-home theory, as the corollary of H0–H2 it is. What attaches to `K.σ` *un*conditionally is only the register-before-allocate dependency — an allocation into `d` presupposes `d ∈ dom(M)`, a genuine `≺`-ordering independent of any freshness mechanism. The account-tier *collision* is the conditional part — the *shared-frontier conditional*, which we state next and cite by that name thereafter.

It arises exactly under realizations that compute a document address from a *shared per-account frontier*, and two bracket the design: Nelson's owned-numbers picture baptises a document by its account (`A_doc`'s emission rule — a document address handed out one tier up the owned-numbers tree exactly as a content address is handed out by its document's `A_{s_C}`), and Gregory's `udanax-green` computes `next_doc_addr = max_child(account) + 1` (the *same* `inc(max,·)` shape as content allocation). Neither is *forced* by the committed stack: `A_doc` is ASN-0047's allocator over an entity set `E` that `𝔼`'s `K.σ` does not carry, so we cannot derive `A_doc`-conformance for `𝔼`'s documents — which is exactly why the dependence is conditional rather than a theorem of `𝔼`. Under *any* such **shared-frontier realization** — each a function of the account's current document population — H0/H1/H2 carry over with the **account as home** and its document allocator standing in for `A_{s_C}`: two same-account creations against a common pre-state read one frontier and so compute one document address (H2 one tier up), while creations under distinct accounts commute (H1). A realization that instead hands out distinct fresh document addresses *without* reading a shared population-max — a **collision-free scheme** — satisfies bare freshness with no same-account contention and incurs no such obligation. So §9's account-tier clause-2 obligation binds the shared-frontier family and not the collision-free family: a conditional dependence on the realization, not a property of `𝔼`. The rejection path is the same conditional seen from the freshness side — two agents proposing the *same* fresh `d` both pass `d ∉ dom(M)`, the first commits and the second is *rejected* (A1); per-account serialization of the document frontier is what instead hands them consecutive document slots, both fresh and neither refused (H2 one tier up, SAFE(c) read one tier up).

Lifting confluence to cover `K.σ` needs one further commutation, the registration analog of H1.

> **H3 (RegistrationIndependence).** Under the shared-frontier conditional of §4, a `K.σ` registering `d_new` *commutes* with every `→_sh` step except two `≺`-comparable ones it must be sequenced against: an allocation into `d_new` itself, and a same-account `K.σ` racing it for the same document-frontier slot. *(a)* Against an allocation `K.α`/`K.λ_sh` into a home `d ≠ d_new`: `K.σ` writes only `dom(M)` (adjoining `d_new`) and frames the stores (`C' = C ∧ L' = L`), while the allocation writes only `dom(C)`/`dom(L)` and frames `M` — disjoint components — and each precondition survives the other (the allocation's `d ∈ dom(M)` under `dom(M)`'s growth, `K.σ`'s `d_new ∉ dom(M)` under the allocation's frame on `M`). *(b)* Against a `K.σ` registering `d'_new ≠ d_new`: the two targets being distinct, each adjoins a distinct fresh document and frames the stores, and `d'_new ≠ d_new` preserves both freshness preconditions across the swap. The two *non*-commuting pairs are `K.σ(d_new)` against an allocation into `d_new`, whose `d_new ∈ dom(M)` holds only *after* the registration (the register-before-allocate dependency), and a same-account `K.σ` pair reading one document frontier (the account-tier H2 collision); both `≺`-comparable, neither ever transposed.
>
> *Proof.* The two cases commute for *different* structural reasons, and folding them together would misdescribe (b). *(a)* is genuinely a disjoint-write commutation — the registration analog of H1's disjoint-state argument: `K.σ` writes only `dom(M)` while the allocation writes only a store and frames `M`, so the two touch disjoint components, each precondition surviving the other (as the statement details). Here `dom(M)` plays the role a sub-allocator chain plays in H1 — a component one step writes and the other does not. *(b)* is *not* a disjoint-write: both `K.σ` steps *read* `dom(M)` (the precondition `d_new ∉ dom(M)`) *and write* `dom(M)` (inserting their target), sharing read-and-write access to the *one* component `dom(M)` — there is no second chain to separate them the way H1 has two. They commute instead by *distinct-element non-interference*: a membership-test-and-insert of `d_new` and one of `d'_new` do not interfere precisely because the targets are distinct (the case hypothesis `d'_new ≠ d_new`), so each step's `dom(M)`-read verdict and inserted element are unchanged by the other's insertion, and both freshness preconditions survive the swap. ∎

With H3 the schedule model and its confluence lift to full executions. Augment `G-PO`'s `O` to admit `K.σ` steps under the two `≺`-constraints H3 names — *register-before-allocate* always, and *per-account serialization* of same-account `K.σ` (the account-tier instance of clause 2, under §4's shared-frontier conditional) — leaving registration/allocation pairs into distinct homes, and registration/registration pairs into distinct documents, `≺`-incomparable. H3 supplies the one commutation G1's confluence still lacked, and G1 then extends verbatim, its `≺`-incomparable transpositions now of three kinds — allocation/allocation into distinct `(d, S)` (H1), registration/allocation into distinct homes, and registration/registration into distinct documents (both H3). Confluence to one committed final state therefore holds for the *full* execution, not merely its allocation fragment: per-home serialization, the account tier of clause 2 included, suffices even when documents are registered mid-execution.

G1 is the practical payload, and it is a claim about a *fixed* schedule `O` of raw steps. An implementer is *not* obliged to commit a total schedule — Gregory's single global loop is one such, and a maximally serial one. They may instead commit a schedule that forces an order *only within each home*, leaving agents in different homes `≺`-incomparable and so free to proceed with no coordination whatsoever; by G1(i) every interleaving of `O` the runtime then produces is a valid execution, and by G1(ii) they all reach the same committed state — for that fixed set of raw steps, observationally indistinguishable, for all of the substrate's invariants, from the global order the axiom postulates. Gregory's loop over-satisfies the requirement; per-home serialization is the least that meets it.

One seam between *steps* and *operations* must be marked before the contract leans on G1. G1(ii)'s confluence is proven for a fixed `(O, ≺)` of raw `→_sh` steps, and at that level it is exact: `K.λ_sh` does no de-duplication, so two same-value deposits into distinct homes are distinct steps that commute (H1). But an `Emit_K` *operation* under `idem(K) = ⊤` is realized either as a step or as a zero-step hit, and which one depends on the *global* active subset `A_K` at the moment it runs (ASN-0128 I1, whose dedup ranges over all of `A_K`, not per-home). So the operation→schedule realization is itself order-dependent — in two distinct *families*, which we take in turn. *The first family — the active-membership toggle.* A lone `idem = ⊤` `Emit_K`'s hit/miss verdict reads the *global* active subset `A_K`, never a per-home slice (ASN-0128 I1), so *any* concurrent operation that flips whether a coverage-equal tuple is *active* flips that verdict with it — cross-home *or* same-home, since the read it races is global either way — and the survivor, the realized step count, and the committed state turn on the race. Two instances populate this family, one toggling membership *on* and one *off*.

*Instance (i) — a competing coverage-equal emit.* Let two agents in homes `d` and `d'` both call `Emit_K(F, G, K)` with `idem(K) = ⊤` and coverage-equal `(F, G)`, the hit/miss branch reading the *global* `A_K`. In the order `X;Y`, X deposits at `a_emit(Σ, d)` and Y is a hit returning X's address; in `Y;X`, Y deposits at `a_emit(Σ, d')` and X is a hit. Both orders presuppose that the second emit's dedup-read sees the first's deposit — that the dedup-read-and-deposit is serialized across the coverage class, the very "serializing authority [that] orders the two calls before either becomes a step" on which ASN-0128 I4's single-survivor explicitly rests. *That authority is global, not per-home*, and per-home MIC (clauses 1–6) does not supply it.

Drop it and a third interleaving opens: X reads the global `A_K` against `Σ` (no match → miss), Y reads it against the *same stale* `Σ` (no match → miss), then each deposits at its frontier, and the post-state carries *two* coverage-equal active `K`-tuples. The both-miss interleaving requires only that the two deposits land at *distinct* addresses, which holds at either home arrangement: *cross-home* (`d ≠ d'`) by H1 — distinct origins, `a_emit(Σ, d) ≠ a_emit(Σ, d')`, no allocation collision; *same-home* (`d = d'`, two coverage-equal `idem = ⊤` emits into one `(d, s_L)` — the *primary* idempotent case, a retry or two workers asserting the same edge in one document) by clause 2's own spacing, which orders only the two *deposits* — landing them at consecutive frontier slots `φ, φ+1` (H0), distinct and both born active — and never reaches the global dedup-read that precedes them. Either way two coverage-equal active tuples stand, and nothing in clauses 1–6 forbids it: clause 4 pins each dedup-read to *some* committed state (here the stale `Σ`), never to its own deposit's pre-state where it would be a hit. Whether ASN-0128 I1a's single-survivor nonetheless excludes the execution turns on a divergence inside I1a itself.

> **G2 (SurfaceEmitDivergence).** I1a's *literal* clause calls a derivation `K`-surface-emitted iff every `L_K`-growing step is "the deposit branch of an `Emit_K` invocation"; the *operative* notion its induction actually uses asks more — each such deposit must be a *miss evaluated at that step's own pre-state* ("at the pre-state its I0-class had no active member"). Under ASN-0128's sequential operation→step identification the two coincide, an operation's dedup-read input state *being* its step's pre-state. A concurrent realization in which an emit's dedup-read and its deposit's pre-state diverge breaks the coincidence: the deposit is a miss against the *stale* state its dedup-read saw yet a hit against its own later pre-state, so the derivation is `K`-surface-emitted under the literal clause but *not* in the operative sense, I1a's induction fails at that deposit, and the `idem = ⊤` duplicate stands.

The divergence names only the state `Σ ∪ {T_X}` and so instantiates identically at both home arrangements above. *Cross-home:* Y's dedup-read fires on the stale `Σ` (a miss) while Y's deposit-step pre-state `Σ ∪ {T_X}` already holds the active `T_X` (a hit there), so I1a's induction breaks at Y's deposit, the two deposits distinct by H1. *Same-home:* identically — `Σ ∪ {T_X}` holds the active `T_X` at Y's deposit-step pre-state regardless of home — with clause 2's slot-spacing, not H1, keeping the deposits distinct. So per-home MIC leaves the derivation operatively *non*-`K`-surface-emitted and the duplicate survives.

So the order-dependence here is not merely in *which* tuple survives — under the two serialized orders the single survivor sits at a *different* chain slot of a *different* home, observably (`Observe_K` returns `a` vs `a'`) — but, absent a global per-coverage-class dedup serialization, in *how many* survive: one under serialization, two under the both-miss interleaving. These two operations are cross-home, hence `≺`-incomparable and free-running, so per-home serialization offers no remedy — it does not even *prevent the duplicate*; only a *global* per-coverage-class serialization of the dedup-read-and-deposit does (§9 clause 7), the one operation-level guarantee that demands a non-per-home discipline.

*Instance (ii) — a nullify of the active incumbent.* The same verdict flips with no second emit in sight, when a concurrent retraction *removes* the very incumbent a lone emit would deduplicate against. Fix a state `Σ` carrying an active incumbent `T = (a_T, F_T, G_T) ∈ A_K^Σ`, `idem(K) = ⊤`; let agent A call `Emit_K(F, G)` at home `d_A` with `(F, G)` coverage-equal to `T`, and agent B call `Nullify_Binary(a_T)` at home `d_B`, targeting the *incumbent* — resident at `Σ`, so `P-tgt` holds by residence in *either* order, and B fires in both. Choose `d_A`, `d_B`, `home(T)` pairwise distinct, so A and B are cross-home and `≺`-incomparable. In the order `A;B`: A reads `A_K^Σ` with `T` active → *hit*, zero steps, returns `a_T` (ASN-0128 I1); then B nullifies `a_T`. Final: class `K` has *no* active tuple, and `dom(L)` gained only B's emitter. In the order `B;A`: B nullifies `a_T` first, so at A's state `T` is gone from `A_K` (ASN-0128 I2) → A *misses* and deposits a fresh active `A' = (a_A, F, G)` at `a_emit(·, d_A)` — `a_A` lying on `d_A`'s chain, outside the unit-depth scope `{a_T}` of B's retraction, so it is born active, not nullified. Final: class `K` has active `A'`, and `dom(L)` gained B's emitter *and* `a_A`. The committed states differ — `Observe_K(F-pattern, oper)` returns `∅` versus `{A'}`, the realized step count 1 versus 2. The coordination disciplines do not remove this instability: emit-before-retract is vacuous here — `T` was emitted long before B, so it holds in *both* orders and constrains nothing — and clause 7 (§9) serializes only racing coverage-equal *emits*, so it does not reach this lone-emit instance. The order-instability persists under both. So the toggle is the general fact: instance (i) toggles a coverage-equal tuple's membership *on*, instance (ii) toggles it *off*, and either flips a lone `idem = ⊤` emit's verdict precisely because that verdict reads the global `A_K`.

*The second family — the target-residence race.* This source turns not on de-duplication but on a retraction's target precondition `P-tgt`, which reads `A_rel = dom(L)` rather than the active subset `A_K` — so `idem` governs it not at all. Let agent A call `Emit_K` at home `d`, landing — deterministically, hence predictably — at `a = a_emit(Σ, d)` (`FrontierUnification`), and let agent B call `Nullify_Binary(a)` at a *different* home `d_retr = d' ≠ d`; both are proposed against the common `Σ`, cross-home and `≺`-incomparable. In the order `A;B`, A deposits its tuple at `a`, so at B's state `a ∈ A_rel`, `P-tgt` holds, B fires its `Emit_R`, and `a` is *nullified*. In the order `B;A`, at B's state `a ∉ dom(L)` and `a ≠ a_emit(Σ, d')` (B's home is `d'`, not `d`), so `P-tgt` *fails* and B is *rejected* — no step (ASN-0128 S3; A1's rejection case) — after which A deposits at `a`, leaving `a` *active*. The committed final state differs, nullified versus active, by cross-home order alone, with no `idem = ⊤` coverage-equal pair anywhere in it: A is type `K`, B is type `R`, and their `(F, G)` are not coverage-equal. The seam is the same operation→schedule realization one (A1), now seen from the rejection side rather than the hit side: fix the raw set `O = {A's step, B's step}` with *both* firing and it is confluent — B's retraction tuple covers `a`, `a ∈ dom(L)`, so `a` is born nullified whichever step commits first (H1), exactly as G1(ii) promises — but whether B *is in `O`* at all, i.e. whether B realizes as a step or is rejected to zero (A1), is itself the order-dependent fact. The coordination layer's *emit-before-retract* discipline removes it — order every retraction after the emission of its target, and order `B;A` is forbidden, the outcome determinate; absent that hypothesis the race is real, and the substrate's response to the losing order is a clean rejection (ASN-0128 S3; A1's rejection case), never a dangling retraction — so the active slice `A_K = L_K ∖ nullified` (W3) stays coherent under either resolution of the race, `P-tgt` being evaluated at the nullify's own linearization state.

## 5. The invariant partition: what the model keeps, what serialization keeps

We can now answer, invariant by invariant, the question of which guarantees are intrinsic to the model and which are bought only by serialization. The discriminator is the conflict analysis of §4: an invariant is *model-intrinsic* if per-step atomicity (A0) alone preserves it under arbitrary interleaving; it is *serialization-borne* if it additionally requires the per-home ordering of H0/H2.

> **W0 (MonotonicityStepLocal).** Append-only monotonicity and value-immutability — `C0` (ContentImmutability) and `L12` (LinkImmutability), together with document-set monotonicity (ASN-0093's `M1`, ArrangementMonotonicity, `dom(M) ⊆ dom(M')`) — are *model-intrinsic*: their preservation across every step of `𝔼` (A6's transition clause) needs *only* per-step atomicity (A0) and no per-home ordering. Under any interleaving of atomic steps, `dom(C)`, `dom(L)`, and `dom(M)` only grow and existing values never change.

> **W1 (ChainContiguityStructural).** Dense chain contiguity — `ChainMembershipForOrigin`'s gapless initial segment `P_S(d, ·) ⊆ A_S(d)`, with `L-ContiguousPrefix` its link-store form — is *model-intrinsic* (its preservation under every valid step carried by A6). The failure mode without per-home serialization is therefore a *collision*, never a hole: hole-impossibility is A6's, carried by the `inc(max,·)` allocator's gaplessness preservation under any interleaving. A stale frontier read runs the *other* way, to a lower already-filled slot, so two same-home emissions reading one frontier compute the *same* address (H2) and one is rejected to zero (A1) — shrinking the realized population but never perforating it. What per-home serialization buys is thus not contiguity but same-home *uniqueness* (H2): that two concurrently-proposed same-home allocations both succeed at distinct slots rather than colliding. Gregory's evidence, read precisely, locates the genuine dependence on a *different* allocator: a counter-style allocator handing out increasing indices without consulting the population "would preserve uniqueness and global monotonicity but lose contiguity whenever allocations interleave" — but that allocator is *not* `inc(max,0)`, which couples deposit to population, and §1 commits `𝔼` to the `inc(max,0)` stack that excludes it. Under that stack contiguity is structural; the one genuinely serialization-borne contiguity claim is W2's run contiguity, not this one.

> **W3 (ActiveSliceStepLocal).** The relationship between the audit slice `L_K` (every type-`K` tuple ever emitted) and the active subset `A_K` is model-intrinsic, and definitionally so: `A_K = L_K ∖ nullified` *by definition* (ASN-0086 `ActiveSubset`), so both are pure functions of the single state read and an `Observe` at index `k` computes them from `Σ_k` mutually consistently (A3) — coherent at every state by construction, under any interleaving. Nullification only grows `nullified` (R6a, RetractionStability), never rewriting or perforating the relation. Needs only A0.

> **W4 (RegistryRaceFree).** The registry is immutable across all runtime steps (`RegistryInvariance` / P1 of ASN-0126, R1 of ASN-0128: `Σ.registry = Σ_init.registry` at every reachable state). No step in `K` writes it. Hence the question "may two agents writing the same registry interleave?" has *no realization*: registry writes are confined to the single, pre-execution construction of `Σ_init` (`R-VAL`), which is not a step of `𝔼`. Runtime registry access is pure read of an immutable structure — always consistent, never torn, by definition. The write-write race is *vacuously absent*.

W4 closes the "writing the same registry" horn of the question outright: there is no concurrent registry writing because there is no runtime registry writing. Registration is a single-authority construction act, and its immutability is load-bearing for the entire shape/type framework above it. MIC therefore needs no registry-write clause of its own: "registry writes occur only in the construction of `Σ_init`, never at runtime" is a directive *derivable from W4*, recorded for an implementer's convenience but carrying no obligation beyond W4 itself.

Collecting the partition: monotonicity (W0), chain contiguity (W1), the active/audit relationship (W3), registry stability (W4), and the structural canonicity of every state (A6) are *kept by the model itself*. Two uniqueness facts the conflict lemmas already settle join them here as classifications. *Cross-home — and cross-subspace — address uniqueness is model-intrinsic*: by H1 the deposits are distinct independent of order, across both axes (`d ≠ d'` and `d = d'`), uniformly across subspaces, and it needs no serialization at all. *Same-home `(d, S)` uniqueness is serialization-borne*: by H2 two such allocations collide on one address unless serialized within `(d, S)` — as is run contiguity (W2, §6), a per-run rather than per-step strengthening. Nothing on the list requires *global* serialization — which is precisely G1's conclusion, now seen invariant by invariant.

## 6. The contiguous run

A higher layer may ask for something the bare frontier discipline does not give: that a definition's content occupy *one contiguous run* of addresses, even as other agents allocate. We must locate this requirement exactly, because it is stronger than W1 and weaker than a global lock.

Be careful to separate two contiguities. *Chain* contiguity (W1) says `P_S(d, ·)` is a gapless prefix of `A_S(d)` — every frontier slot, whoever filled it, is occupied. It holds on every valid execution regardless of *which agent owns which slot* (W1 is model-intrinsic). *Run* contiguity is the stronger property a definition needs: that *this definition's* atoms occupy a *consecutive* block of slots, with no foreign atom wedged among them. Chain contiguity is about the chain having no holes; run contiguity is about *one author's* atoms forming an unbroken interval *within* the chain.

> **W2 (RunContiguityCritical).** A multi-atom content run for a definition in home `d` (a batch of `K.α` steps into `(d, s_C)`) occupies a contiguous block of `A_{s_C}(d)` iff no foreign allocation into `(d, s_C)` is interleaved between the run's first and last step. This is a *per-`(home, subspace)` critical section spanning the run* — strictly stronger than the per-step per-home serialization that secures same-home uniqueness (H2), strictly weaker than any cross-home exclusion.
>
> *Proof.* By H0 each `s_C`-allocation to `d` lands at the then-current frontier and advances it by one. If the run's `m` steps are the only `s_C`-allocations to `d` in the index interval they span, they land at consecutive slots `φ, φ+1, …, φ+m−1`: a contiguous block. If a foreign `s_C`-allocation to `d` lands at some interior index, it consumes a frontier slot between two of the run's atoms, and the run's remaining atoms land *past* it — the run's slots are `{φ, …}` with a foreign slot embedded, no longer an interval. By H1, a foreign allocation into any *other* `(d', S')` leaves `φ_{s_C}(d, ·)` fixed and cannot break the run. ∎

So the run's *contiguity* requirement is sharply scoped: hold a critical section on `(d, s_C)` for the run's duration; let every other home and the link subspace proceed freely. This is the one place the corpus genuinely needs *batch* contiguity (A5's general denial notwithstanding), and W2 says exactly how much exclusion buys it — no more than a single sub-allocator, for the length of one definition. Contiguity is all W2 buys: a `(d, s_C)` critical section excludes foreign *writers*, keeping the run an unbroken interval of `A_{s_C}(d)`, but it does **not** construct atomicity. Even a perfectly contiguous run *passes through* every interior state `Σ_{i₁+1}, …, Σ_{i₁+m−1}`, each canonical (A6) and carrying a strict prefix of the run's atoms — a partial batch *structurally*, which the writer-side section leaves intact for a reader to land in, whether or not any modeled read witnesses it (for a content run, §8's link-only surface witnesses none; a link-store batch makes the same partiality read-visible, A5). Contiguity is thus the writer-side half of read-atomicity and no more (A5); the reader-side half — making a multi-step batch appear all-or-nothing to an observer — is the harder additional contract we defer to Open Question 4.

Here the abstract model and Gregory's code genuinely diverge. In the *abstract* model, content and links inhabit disjoint subspaces `s_C` and `s_L` with separate frontiers (`DisjointSubAllocatorChains`), so a link allocation to `d` *cannot* break a content run's contiguity — it advances `φ_{s_L}(d)`, not `φ_{s_C}(d)`. In Gregory's *implementation*, link orgls and text atoms share one granfilade allocation, so a `CREATELINK` interleaved into a text run *does* break text contiguity (his "insert-link-insert I-address gap"). The abstract claim W2 is the cleaner one and is the contract we specify: content run contiguity is threatened *only* by a concurrent content allocation to the same home. An implementation that, like Gregory's, fuses the subspaces inherits a coarser exclusion obligation; an implementation faithful to the subspace partition does not.

## 7. A worked allocation scenario

The analysis of §4 and §6 runs entirely on a generic `(d, S)`. It is worth grounding it once in explicit addresses — both to make the conflict structure concrete and to exhibit the exact collision a missing serialization produces.

Fix a node `[1]`, an account `[1.0.1]` under it, and two documents under that account:

`d = [1.0.1.0.1]`,  `d' = [1.0.1.0.2]`  — each with `zeros(·) = 2`, hence valid document homes.

By `FirstEmission` (ASN-0093) the content sub-allocator `A_{s_C}(d)` has anchor `b_C(d) = [d.0.s_C] = [1.0.1.0.1.0.1]` (with `s_C = 1`) and enumerates the content slots

`a_k := [d.0.s_C.k] = [1.0.1.0.1.0.1.k]`,  `k = 1, 2, 3, …`,

each successor `a_{k+1} = inc(a_k, 0)` advancing the final digit. Write `a'_k := [d'.0.s_C.k] = [1.0.1.0.2.0.1.k]` for `d'`'s content chain. Every `a_k` has `zeros(a_k) = 3` and `origin(a_k) = d`, as a content address must.

*Same-home collision (H2, interior).* Suppose `φ_{s_C}(d, Σ) = 3` — slots `a_1, a_2, a_3` are filled — and two writers `w₁, w₂` both propose a content allocation into `d` against this *same* `Σ`. By `SubsequentEmissionFreshness` each reads `a_prev = max P_{s_C}(d, Σ) = a_3` and computes its deposit as `inc(a_3, 0) = a_4 = [1.0.1.0.1.0.1.4]`. *Both compute the identical address `a_4`.* Whichever commits first deposits `a_4`; the second now finds `a_4 ∈ dom(C)`, so its freshness precondition `a_4 ∉ dom_{s_C}` fails (H2). Absent a discipline forcing the two to read distinct frontiers, they collide on one address.

*Same-home collision (H2, first-emission boundary).* The same collision occurs at `φ_{s_C}(d, Σ) = 0`. With `P_{s_C}(d, Σ) = ∅`, `max ∅` is undefined and the subsequent rule does not apply; instead both writers fire the first-emission predicate `{a' : origin(a') = d} = ∅` and both compute the determinate first slot `a_1 = [1.0.1.0.1.0.1.1]` (`FirstEmission`). They collide on `a_1` exactly as the interior case collides on `a_4`, now against `FirstEmissionFreshness`.

*Cross-home commutation (H1).* Now let `w₁` allocate into `d` and `w₂` into `d'`, against the same `Σ` with `φ_{s_C}(d, Σ) = 3` and `φ_{s_C}(d', Σ) = 0`. Then `w₁` lands `a_4 = [1.0.1.0.1.0.1.4]` (with `origin(a_4) = d`) and `w₂` lands `a'_1 = [1.0.1.0.2.0.1.1]` (with `origin(a'_1) = d'`). Because `d ≠ d'` the two deposits carry distinct origins, hence are distinct (H1) — here the homes are *sibling*, so the anchors `b_C(d) = [1.0.1.0.1.0.1]` and `b_C(d') = [1.0.1.0.2.0.1]` differ visibly at the document digit, and H1's origin argument settles distinctness directly. By H0 neither writer's frontier read is disturbed by the other. Applying the two in either order — `w₁` then `w₂`, or `w₂` then `w₁` — reaches the identical final state: the steps commute (H1). No coordination between the two writers is needed or useful.

*Run fragmentation (W2).* Finally let one author issue a 3-atom content run into `(d, s_C)` from frontier `φ_{s_C}(d) = 3`, intending the contiguous block `a_4, a_5, a_6`. If the run's three `K.α` steps are the only `s_C`-allocations to `d` in the index interval they span, they land exactly at `a_4, a_5, a_6` — one interval. But suppose a foreign `K.α` into `(d, s_C)` interleaves after the run's first atom: the run deposits `a_4`, the foreign step takes the next frontier slot `a_5 = [1.0.1.0.1.0.1.5]`, and the run's remaining two atoms land *past* it at `a_6, a_7`. The author's run now occupies `{a_4, a_6, a_7}` — fragmented, the foreign `a_5` wedged inside (W2). A foreign allocation into the *link* subspace `(d, s_L)`, or into any *other* home, advances a different frontier (H1) and cannot fragment the run — which is exactly the scope W2 claims for the critical section: `(d, s_C)`, and nothing wider.

These four vignettes are the whole conflict theory in miniature: collisions live at a single `(home, subspace)` (the `a_4`/`a_1` clashes), everything cross-home commutes (the `a_4` vs `a'_1` independence), and a multi-atom run needs exclusion on its own `(home, subspace)` for its duration and on nothing else (the `a_5` intrusion). Under MIC clause 2 the collisions vanish: serializing the two `d`-writers makes `w₁` read frontier 3 and deposit `a_4`, then `w₂` read frontier 4 and deposit `a_5` — distinct, fresh, and gapless (SAFE(c)).

## 8. Quiescence as a snapshot predicate

We come to the sharpest of the questions, and the one Nelson answered most directly: what does a quiescence verdict mean if it is read while a write is in flight? A higher layer wants quiescence — "is everyone done editing?" — recognizable from inside the predicate language even as writers remain active. We must say when such a verdict is *sound* and when it is a *race*.

A quiescence predicate `Q` is a predicate over states; a verdict is its truth value at the index its `Observe` reads. Two things must be settled. The *referent*: against what state is the verdict taken? And — forced on us by the operation surface — the *read count*: how many `Observe` calls a verdict costs, because the substrate exposes no whole-state read, only the per-type `Observe_K` (ASN-0086; ASN-0128 D1–D4 / BH1–BH4). A predicate confined to one type is one `Observe_K`; a predicate over several types or homes — the realistic quiescence condition, and ASN-0128's `targets_keyed` already *joins across every Binary type* — is several. We must therefore be explicit about how a multi-read `Q` is *computed* from the surface the substrate actually exposes, because the soundness argument turns on it. With no whole-state read available, a `p`-read verdict is realized as

`Q = g(c₁(Σ_{r₁}), …, c_p(Σ_{r_p}))`

for a combining function `g` over the `p` *bounded-access constituents* `c₁, …, c_p`, each `c_i` a single-state read taken at its own read index `r_i`, the indices non-decreasing (`r₁ ≤ ⋯ ≤ r_p`, since zero-step reads do not advance `𝔼`). The constituents are deliberately *not* uniformly `Observe_K`-grade, and that generality is forced by `stale`: a cross-type join realizes each `c_i` as one type's active-view read `Observe_{K_i}(oper)`, but a `stale` enumeration realizes its constituents as a *single* active-view read `Observe_K(oper)` — global, since active-membership `a ∉ nullified(Σ)` consults the *global* retraction slice `L_R^Σ`, whose nullifying emitters may be homed at any `d_retr ≠ d` and so escape a frontier descent of `d`'s own link subspace — together with *one per-home frontier descent* `f_{d_i}` per distinct member home, a frontier descent being a bounded access that reads a home's cross-type link frontier rather than any one type's active view. So `stale` is a multi-read whenever its active slice is non-empty: at member-home count `N ≥ 1` it costs `N + 1 ≥ 2` bounded accesses (the single-home case `1 + 1 = 2` included), hence clause 6's province; only the degenerate empty-slice case `A_K^Σ = ∅` (`N = 0`) collapses to a single `Observe_K(oper)` returning `∅`, one bounded access that clause 4 discharges trivially. The cross-type join has the same boundary — a `targets_keyed` join over a *single* registered BH3 type is one access, clause 4's, and only a join over two or more types is clause 6's. Thus `c_i` ranges over both kinds — active-view reads and frontier descents alike — and the soundness argument below must be stated over the `c_i`, not over `Observe_{K_i}`, on pain of leaving the frontier constituents ungoverned. `g` is left *arbitrary*: a conjunction `⋀_i Q_{K_i}` is one special case, but a verdict that joins across types (a `targets_keyed`-style cross-type condition) or recovers a per-home quantity from a frontier constituent (`stale`'s per-member `age(a) = f_d^Σ − 1 − j` at `d = home(a)`) is an equally admissible `g`, and we do *not* assume `Q` decomposes as a conjunction. Define a writer step to be **`Q`-affecting**, relative to a read window, when it changes the value of some constituent `c_i` whose read has not yet been taken — i.e. `c_i` evaluated just before the step differs from `c_i` just after, for some such `i`. The quantifier ranges over *every* constituent, frontier descents included: a step that advances a not-yet-read frontier `f_{d_i}` *is* `Q`-affecting even when it disturbs no active view — exactly what a `K' ≠ K` link emit into member home `d_i` does, advancing `d_i`'s cross-type link frontier (BH4) while leaving every `A_K` fixed. A step that alters no not-yet-read constituent `c_i` in the window leaves every still-pending argument of `g` untouched, hence leaves the verdict `g(…)` untouched — whatever `g` is. The single-read and multi-read cases diverge on exactly the soundness premise, so we take them in turn (V0, then V2); durability (V1) follows.

> **V0 (VerdictSnapshotLocal).** A verdict obtained by a *single bounded access* — a single `Observe_K` (a predicate on one type's active view), or equally an `age` read of one home's single-state frontier `f_d` — is `Q(Σ_r)` for the one index `r` that access reads (A3's realized register, under clause 4): it is a sound statement *about the one state* `Σ_r`, and about no `Σ_{r'}` with `r' ≠ r`. The discriminator is *access count*, not type-confinement: what makes the verdict snapshot-local is that its lone access lands on one index, whether that access reads a type's active view or a home's cross-type frontier. That `age` qualifies is the one case needing an argument: `age(a) = f_d^Σ − 1 − j` reads not a type's active view but the *home* `d = home(a)`'s link-subspace frontier `f_d^Σ`, which interleaves *every* link type homed at `d` (BH4) — *not* an `Observe_K`-grade read. Yet it is single-*index*: by `L-ContiguousPrefix` (ASN-0086) the home's link population is a contiguous initial segment of the *one* chain `chain_d`, so `f_d^Σ` is simply its length, recovered in one bounded access over that home's link subspace (Gregory's `findpreviousisagr` — a single bounded granfilade descent under the home's link-subspace upper bound), never by unioning per-type views at drifting indices, of which there are none. So `age` is a *home-relative single-snapshot* read — one index, free under clause 4 like the single-type reads. Because every state on `𝔼` is structurally canonical (A6), `Σ_r` is always a coherent referent — there is always a well-defined "single frozen view" to evaluate `Q` against.

The reason a one-index verdict is *trustworthy* and a many-index verdict is *accidental* is exact: if you evaluate different constituent reads of `Q` across an intervening edit — one `Observe_{K_i}` against `Σ_{r₁}` *before* writer A's edit, another `Observe_{K_j}` against a later `Σ_{r₂}` *after* writer B's edit — you have quantified over a collection of states with no common index, feeding `g` arguments that never held together, and may report "everyone done" for a configuration that obtained at *no single instant*. A condition that *secures* soundness is therefore stark and simple: **let all of the verdict's reads occur at one index** — the verdict is then literally `Q(Σ_r)`. This is not the *weakest* such condition (V2 sharpens it: soundness in fact needs only that no *`Q`-affecting* step fall between the reads), but one index is the stronger, conveniently constructible condition that MIC clause 6 adopts. For a single `Observe_K` this is free — one read, hence one index — and A6 guarantees that snapshot is always a real, canonical state, A3 that it is read whole, A0 that a concurrent write cannot smear it. So a single-read verdict taken "while a write is in flight" is *never corrupted by the write*: the in-flight write linearizes at some index `j`, and relative to `Σ_r` it is either already counted (`j < r`, its effect is in the snapshot — it is *committed*, not "in flight," from the snapshot's vantage) or not yet counted (`j ≥ r`, its effect is absent). The atomicity of the step (A0) forbids any "half-counted" writer. This is what it means for the verdict to be a snapshot rather than a race: it is the truth about the prefix of the order up to `r`.

The multi-read verdict keeps that *same* premise — all reads at one index — but no longer gets it for free, and here the per-type surface forces a seam into the open, mirroring §4's step/operation seam. A multi-read `Q` is realized as `p` bounded-access constituent reads `c₁, …, c_p` combined by `g` (above) — for a cross-type join, `p` active-view reads `Observe_{K₁}, …, Observe_{K_p}`; for a `stale` enumeration, one active-view read `Observe_K(oper)` plus one frontier descent `f_{d_i}` per member home — each read zero-step (A1). Zero-step reads do not advance `𝔼` — the index moves only when a *writer* step linearizes — so the `p` reads observe one state iff no foreign step at all interleaves among them, and possibly several — between 2 and `p` — distinct states otherwise (one interleaved foreign step splits the reads across exactly two states, not `p`; the count of distinct observed states climbs toward `p` only as further foreign steps fall between consecutive reads). But "all reads at one index" and "the verdict is sound" are not the same condition, and we must not run them together. Soundness asks less — a weaker condition suffices: that no *`Q`-affecting* step — one that changes the value of some not-yet-read constituent `c_i` (above) — linearizes between the first and last read. A foreign writer step that is *not* `Q`-affecting advances the index, scattering the reads across distinct `Σ_{r₁}, …, Σ_{r_p}`, yet cannot corrupt the verdict — and the reason is the one the definition makes exact, *not* that the step "leaves every constituent reading identically." It need not: a non-`Q`-affecting step is free to change an *already-read* constituent (the trace below exhibits exactly this — a `K₁`-emit that flips `A_{K₁}` *after* `K₁` has been read), merely *banking* the value already captured. What it cannot touch is a *not-yet-read* constituent, by the very definition of `Q`-affecting. Run that out per constituent against the first read's index `r₁`: for each `i`, `c_i` is not-yet-read throughout `[r₁, r_i)`, so any step in that interval that changed `c_i` would *be* `Q`-affecting; since none is, `c_i` is constant on `[r₁, r_i]` and the captured value is `v_i = c_i(Σ_{r_i}) = c_i(Σ_{r₁})` (changes to `c_i` *after* `r_i` are immaterial — `v_i` is banked). This holds for every `i` — for a `stale` enumeration's frontier constituents `c_i = f_{d_i}` no less than for active-view constituents — so `g(v₁, …, v_p) = g(c₁(Σ_{r₁}), …, c_p(Σ_{r₁})) = Q(Σ_{r₁})`: the verdict is sound *about the first read's state* `Σ_{r₁}`, the one index every banked value traces back to. Only when a `Q`-affecting step strides between the reads does the verdict go wrong inside the recognizer's *own* evaluation: some `c_i` taken against `Σ_{r_i}` *before* the affecting step and some `c_j` against a later `Σ_{r_j}` *after* it, so `g` is applied to a tuple of constituent views that held at no single index. So a sound multi-read verdict is, for `p > 1`, not a primitive the surface supplies but a *construction obligation* with a determinate shape:

> **V2 (VerdictReaderSnapshot).** For a multi-read verdict — `p ≥ 2` constituent bounded-access reads composing one `Q`, whether `Observe_K` calls across types or the active-view read and per-home frontier descents of a `stale` enumeration (§8) — three conditions stand in a chain of *strict* implications, not in equivalence:
>
> `[all p reads at one committed index Σ_r] ⟹ [no Q-affecting step linearizes between the first and last read] ⟹ [the verdict is sound about a single state].`
>
> The middle condition — *no `Q`-affecting step between the reads* — is the weakest *sufficient* condition this note establishes, **not** a necessary one: the implication to soundness is genuinely *strict*, since a verdict may come out sound by other routes — `g` insensitive to the disturbed constituent, or `g`'s value coinciding with `Q` of some single state — even when a `Q`-affecting step does fall between the reads (the banking argument above proves only the sufficient direction, no `Q`-affecting step ⟹ verdict `= Q(Σ_{r₁})`, and offers nothing converse). Each implication's converse fails — the first because a non-`Q`-affecting step scatters the reads across distinct indices yet leaves soundness intact, the second because soundness survives when `g` discards the disturbed constituent — and the prose below exhibits a concrete witness for each, alongside the trace. The first — *all reads at one index* — is *strictly* stronger: a non-`Q`-affecting step falsifies it while preserving soundness. We single it out not because soundness needs it but because a reader-side critical section conveniently *supplies* it, and MIC clause 6 *adopts* it for safety — pinning all `p` reads to one index discharges the soundness requirement a fortiori, sparing the recognizer from having to classify which steps are `Q`-affecting. The one-index construction is *constructible* (hold the exclusion, take the `p` reads, release: they then all read one `Σ_r`) but *not automatic* from the per-type `Observe_K`, which pins each call to its own index and no further. The substrate neither provides nor forbids it.

This is a *reader-side* obligation, dual to W2's writer-side run contiguity *in role but not in scope*: W2 holds a *single* `(d, s_C)` against foreign *writer* steps for a run's duration so the run stays one interval — a *local* exclusion, strictly weaker than any cross-home one (§6); V2's one-index construction holds the read sequence against *every* interleaving writer step for the verdict's duration — and that is *global*, because any writer step at any home advances the index off which the `p` reads must all agree. The roles are dual (writer-side vs reader-side); the scopes are not (local vs global). Even V2's *weaker* sufficient condition — no `Q`-affecting step between the reads — is not per-home: a `Q`-affecting step is any that moves a *not-yet-read* constituent `c_i`, and which homes it must exclude depends on the constituent's kind. For a cross-type join each constituent is an active-view read `Observe_{K_i}`, `c_i = A_{K_i} = L_{K_i} ∖ nullified`, so a step is `Q`-affecting when it moves a not-yet-read `A_{K_i}` — and that happens *two* ways: a `K_i`-emit growing `L_{K_i}`, *and* a nullification, an `Emit_R` whose to-coverage hits an active `K_i` tuple, growing `nullified` and so shrinking `A_{K_i}`. The first is homed wherever a `K_i` tuple may live; the second is homed at the *retractor's* home `d_retr`, which need bear no relation to `Q`'s types and may be *any* home at all. So the excluded steps are emphatically *not* "the homes carrying `Q`'s types" — that undercounts, dropping exactly the nullifications: they are every `K_i`-emit *together with* every active-`K_i`-hitting `Emit_R`, the latter at an arbitrary retractor home. (The §8 trace below masks this: its lone `Q`-affecting step is a nullify that *happens* to be co-homed with its target, but the general retractor sits anywhere.) For a `stale` enumeration the constituents additionally include the frontier descents `f_{d_i}`, and a frontier `f_{d_i}` is moved by *any* link emit into member home `d_i` whatever its type, so the excluded steps there sweep *every* emit into a member home, not just the type-`K` ones. Either way the exclusion ranges wider than a single `(d, S)`. The two disciplines are different in kind — one governs who may deposit and where, the other what a single logical read may span and against whom — which is why the contract must name both, and why the per-*call* snapshot guarantee (MIC clause 4, A3, free) cannot stand in for the per-*verdict* one (clause 6, V2, constructed). A single-bounded-access verdict — a single `Observe_K`, or an `age` read (V0) — needs only clause 4; the multi-read verdict a real recognizer needs — a cross-type join, or any `stale` enumeration — is sound only under clause 6.

We ground V2 in an explicit trace, in §7's style. Take the two-type quiescence predicate `Q ≡ (A_{K₁} = ∅) ∧ (A_{K₂} = ∅)` — "no active tuple of either type" — realized as `g(v₁, v₂) = [v₁ = ∅ ∧ v₂ = ∅]` over the two reads `v_i = Observe_{K_i}(oper)`. Fix a start state `Σ_n` carrying exactly one active tuple: a `K₂`-tuple `T₂` at link address `a_{T₂} = [1.0.1.0.1.0.2.1]` — the first link slot (`s_L = 2`) of home `d = [1.0.1.0.1]` — with `A_{K₁}^{Σ_n} = ∅`. A recognizer that lets its two reads *drift* across indices realizes the verdict so:

- *Read 1, at `r₁ = n`.* `v₁ = Observe_{K₁}(Σ_{r₁}) = ∅` — no active `K₁`-tuple. The `K₁` constituent is now banked.
- *Foreign step at `r₁`* (`Σ_{r₁} → Σ_{r₁+1}`). A writer emits a `K₁`-tuple `T₁` (`Emit_{K₁}` miss, one `K.λ_sh`), landing at the next link frontier slot `a_{T₁} = inc(a_{T₂}, 0) = [1.0.1.0.1.0.2.2]`. This flips `A_{K₁}` from `∅` to `{T₁}` — but `K₁` was *already read* at `r₁`, so the step is **not** `Q`-affecting: `g`'s first argument stays pinned to `∅`.
- *Foreign step at `r₁+1`* (`Σ_{r₁+1} → Σ_{r₁+2}`). A writer nullifies `T₂` (`Nullify_Binary(a_{T₂})` miss, one `K.λ_sh`), depositing a retraction emitter `b = [1.0.1.0.1.0.2.3]` whose unit-depth to-span covers `a_{T₂}`, so `a_{T₂} ∈ nullified` and `T₂` leaves `A_{K₂}`. `K₂` has *not yet been read*, so this is the lone **`Q`-affecting** step.
- *Read 2, at `r₂ = r₁+2`.* `v₂ = Observe_{K₂}(Σ_{r₂}) = ∅` — `T₂` is now nullified.

The verdict is `g(∅, ∅) = ⊤`: "quiescent." Yet `Q` holds at *no* state the trace touches. At `Σ_{r₁}`: `A_{K₂} = {T₂} ≠ ∅`. At `Σ_{r₁+1}`: `A_{K₁} = {T₁} ≠ ∅` *and* `A_{K₂} = {T₂} ≠ ∅`. At `Σ_{r₂}`: `A_{K₁} = {T₁} ≠ ∅`. The configuration `g` reports — both slices empty — coexisted at no single index: `v₁ = ∅` held at `r₁`, `v₂ = ∅` at `r₂`, never together. This is exactly the "states that never coexisted" error, now manufactured *inside the recognizer's own evaluation*, and V2's strict-implication chain pins its single cause: one `Q`-affecting step (the `T₂`-nullify) strode between the reads. The non-`Q`-affecting `K₁`-emit moved the index and changed a slice without harming the verdict — its constituent read was already banked — which is precisely the slack V2's middle condition tolerates and V2's first condition (all reads at one index) does not.

Two variations on this trace make V2's implications *strict*. *First converse* — `[no Q-affecting step] ⟹ [all reads at one index]`. Strip the trace of its `T₂`-nullify, leaving only the non-`Q`-affecting `K₁`-emit between the two reads. Then `[no Q-affecting step]` holds, yet that emit advances the index so the reads still land at two distinct states — `[all reads at one index]` fails — and by the banking argument the verdict is still sound about `Σ_{r₁}`: it computes to `⊥`, matching `Q(Σ_{r₁}) = ⊥`. So the trace-minus-nullify witnesses `[no Q-affecting] ∧ ¬[one index] ∧ sound`, making the first condition strictly stronger than the middle. (The *full* trace, nullify intact, is not this witness: it contains the `Q`-affecting `T₂`-nullify, so `[no Q-affecting step]` is false in it; what it exhibits is the second implication's necessity — a `Q`-affecting step striding between drifting reads *causes* unsoundness — together with the gap between `[one index]` and `[no Q-affecting]` that its harmless `K₁`-emit occupies.) *Second converse* — `[sound] ⟹ [no Q-affecting step]`. Replace `g` with a *short-circuit* combiner `g(v₁, v₂) = (v₁ ≠ ∅) ∨ (v₂ = ∅)` over two active-view reads, against a start state with `A_{K₁} = {T₁} ≠ ∅`. Read `v₁ = Observe_{K₁}(Σ_{r₁}) = {T₁}` at `r₁`: the first disjunct fires, so `g` short-circuits to `⊤` whatever `v₂` holds, and `Q(Σ_{r₁}) = g({T₁}, ∅) = ⊤`. Now let a writer emit a `K₂`-tuple between the reads, flipping the *not-yet-read* `K₂` constituent `A_{K₂}` from `∅` to `{T₂}` — by definition a *`Q`-affecting* step, so the middle condition **fails**. Yet read 2 returns `v₂ = {T₂}` and the verdict is `g({T₁}, {T₂}) = ⊤ ∨ ⊥ = ⊤ = Q(Σ_{r₁})`: *sound about `Σ_{r₁}`* despite the intervening `Q`-affecting step, because `g`'s short-circuit discards the very constituent the step disturbed. Soundness holds where the middle condition does not — so the second implication, like the first, is strict.

Now run the *original* trace under MIC clause 6. A reader-side critical section pins both reads to one committed index — `Σ_n` — admitting no writer step between them. Then `v₁ = Observe_{K₁}(Σ_n) = ∅` and `v₂ = Observe_{K₂}(Σ_n) = {T₂}`, so `g(∅, {T₂}) = ⊥`: "not quiescent." This is *sound* — it is the truth about `Σ_n`, where `A_{K₂} ≠ ∅` genuinely holds. The two writer steps are not lost; they linearize *after* `r₂` once the section releases, and the verdict, retrospective about `Σ_n` (V1), simply makes no claim about the later states they produce. The drifting reader turned a true "not quiescent" into a false "quiescent"; the pinned reader recovers the sound verdict — which is the whole content of clause 6.

But soundness — V0 for a single read, V2 for several — says nothing about whether the verdict *stays* true, and here we must be honest about what a quiescence verdict can and cannot promise:

> **V1 (VerdictRetrospective).** A verdict `Q(Σ_r)` is retrospective: it asserts `Q` of `Σ_r` and of nothing later. For `r' > r`, `Q(Σ_{r'})` may differ — any step at index `j` with `r ≤ j < r'` may falsify `Q`. Extending a verdict from "held at `r`" to "holds through `r'`" requires an *additional* hypothesis the substrate does not supply: that no `Q`-falsifying step is linearized in the interval `[r, r')`. That hypothesis is a constraint on writers' linearization points relative to the observer's read — a coordination-layer obligation (extinction discipline, fair scheduling, bounded work — the coordination layer's named hypotheses), not a substrate guarantee.

V1 is the precise statement of the relationship the question asks for — what must hold "for the verdict to be sound rather than a race": soundness about `Σ_r` (secured at one read index — V0 for a single bounded access, V2's reader-side construction for several) and durability past `r` (V1's separate hypothesis, which the substrate does not supply) are distinct properties, and conflating them — reading "the system is quiescent and will stay so" off `Q(Σ_r)` — is the smuggling a quiescence layer must refuse.

The substrate's contribution is exactly and only the snapshot. Whether a verdict so obtained licenses *terminating* the system is the separate, conditional question a termination layer answers with its named hypotheses about who may still fire; that snapshot is the "reached-and-held quiescence" referent, and holding it is the layer's job.

## 9. The minimal isolation contract

We can now collect the discipline an implementation must honour. The contract is the conjunction of the clauses we have been forced to by the analysis; nothing in it is a mechanism (no lock, no transaction, no scheduler), only obligations that any mechanism must meet.

> **MIC (MinimalIsolationContract).** A faithful realization of the substrate's consistency model must honour:
>
> 1. **Per-step atomicity (A0).** Each atomic transition is applied indivisibly; no observer reads a state strictly between `Σ_i` and `Σ_{i+1}`.
> 2. **Per-home allocation serialization (H0, H2).** For each `(home, subspace) (d, S)`, the frontier-read-and-deposit of any two `S`-allocations to `d` are mutually exclusive — securing same-home uniqueness (H2); chain contiguity (W1) holds regardless, model-intrinsic. Cross-`(d,S)` allocations need no coordination (H1). Document registration `K.σ` is the account-tier instance of this clause (the shared-frontier conditional, §4/H3).
> 3. **Commit-before-acknowledge (A7).** An operation's response is produced only at or after its linearization point.
> 4. **Per-call snapshot reads (A3, V0).** Each *single-bounded-access* read — an `Observe_K`, or BH4's `age` — is evaluated against a single committed state `Σ_k` (V0). The *several*-access reads — cross-type joins and `stale` — are clause 6's, not this clause's.
> 5. **Per-run critical section for contiguous runs (W2).** A definition's multi-atom content run holds exclusion on its `(d, s_C)` for the run's duration; no foreign content allocation to `d` interleaves.
> 6. **Per-verdict reader snapshot (V2).** A verdict composed of two or more bounded-access reads — several `Observe_K` calls, a cross-type join, or a `stale` enumeration — must pin all its constituent reads to one committed index, via a reader-side critical section against any interleaving writer step. This exclusion is *global*, not per-home (V2). A *single-bounded-access* verdict (V0) discharges this clause for free under clause 4.
> 7. **Per-coverage-class idem=⊤ de-duplication serialization (I1a, I4).** For each coverage class `[K]` of an `idem(K) = ⊤` type, the dedup-read of the *global* active subset `A_K` and the consequent deposit form **one atomic action**, pinned to the operation's linearization index — so an `Emit_K` that misses does so against its *own* deposit's pre-state, never a stale earlier state. Since the dedup-read of `A_K` is global, not per-home (ASN-0128 I1), this is a **non-per-home**, *per-coverage-class* serialization: orthogonal to clause 2 (which still governs the deposit's allocation freshness), and the one *operation*-level guarantee that resists the per-home thesis — *role*-distinct from the allocation clauses 2/5 and the reader-side clause 6. An `idem = ⊥` emit (no dedup) and any allocation discharge it trivially. Drop it and per-home MIC permits the duplicate; the atomic dedup-read-and-deposit is the delta that restores the *operative* `K`-surface-emittedness I1a's induction needs and so suppresses it (G2; §4 instance (i)).

Registration adds no eighth clause — `K.σ` falls under clause 2's account tier (§4). The contract is *minimal*: each of its seven clauses is independently load-bearing — removing any one admits a counterexample (drop 1 and reads tear; drop 2 and same-home allocations collide, same-account document creations included; drop 3 and callers act on phantom effects; drop 4 and a single logical read's internal accesses — an `age` frontier descent, or `Observe_K`'s own `A_K = L_K ∖ nullified` computation — straddle a commit, compounding two committed states into one verdict though clause 1 keeps every step atomic; drop 5 and definition runs fragment; drop 6 and a multi-read verdict quantifies over states that held at no common index; drop 7 and two coverage-equal `idem = ⊤` emits both miss and duplicate).

The contract earns its name by the safety it implies:

> **SAFE (SafetyUnderMIC).** Under MIC, (a) no reader observes a torn effect; (b) no *single operation's* effect is observed twice, and idempotent emission admits no duplicate *under clause 7* (per-home clauses 1–6 alone permit an `idem = ⊤` duplicate, §4 instance (i)) — while non-idempotent emission admits content-equal tuples *by design*, which MIC neither prevents nor intends to; (c) no writer collides with a concurrent writer's *allocation* — a sub-allocator emission `K.α`/`K.λ_sh`, or, under §4's shared-frontier conditional, a `K.σ` into an account's document allocator; and (d) no multi-read verdict straddles a write.
>
> *Proof.* (a) *No torn effect.* Any single emission or nullification is one step; by clauses 1 and 4 the reader's read is single-state (A3) and lands before or after that step, never within (A4). A batch is not a single effect; its constituents are each whole, and clause 4 ensures each read is one canonical snapshot (A6) — fully structurally canonical, never corrupt (though, by §2, possibly mid-batch).
>
> (b) *No duplicated effect — scoped to what is actually proved.* Two facts hold, and a third must be stated plainly so the guarantee is not overclaimed. *(i) Per-operation uniqueness.* Each state-changing operation is realized at one unique index (A1), so *its* effect appears exactly once in the order; no single operation is double-applied. *(ii) Idempotent collapse — under clause 7, and only then.* Under clause 7, I1a applies (G2; §4 instance (i)), so `A_K` holds at most one tuple per coverage class; resurrection-after-nullification is by design (W3, ASN-0128 I2), not a duplicate. Per-home clauses 1–6 alone do not place an `idem = ⊤` execution in I1a's scope — the both-miss interleaving breaks its induction and the duplicate stands (G2) — so the guarantee rests on clause 7, not per-home MIC. That same clause reduces two *concurrent* coverage-equal emissions into distinct homes to operation-level *non-confluence* rather than duplication: first-to-commit (ASN-0128 I4) fixes which home keeps the lone survivor (§4 instance (i)); absent clause 7 the dependence reaches *how many*, the both-miss interleaving leaving two. *(iii) But `idem(K) = ⊥` duplicates by design, and MIC does not prevent it.* Two `Emit_K` calls with identical `(F, G, K)` under `idem(K) = ⊥` produce *distinct* addresses, and *both* tuples appear in the active subset (ASN-0128 I5; ASN-0086 R2's consequence) — a reader observing `A_K` then sees two tuples of identical content. This is intended: a fresh non-idempotent emission is how the layer expresses a genuinely new instance, and no clause of MIC forbids it. In particular, commit-before-acknowledge (clause 3 / A7) does *not* suppress it: A7 fixes only that a response is produced at-or-after `lin(op)`; it neither detects nor cancels a *lost* acknowledgment, so a client that times out and retries a non-idempotent emission commits a second tuple, and A7 is indifferent to the fact. What collapses semantic repeats is idempotency (ii), not acknowledgment ordering. MIC's (b) guarantee is therefore exactly (i)+(ii). The reference-sharing principle — one permanent address, never a copy (ASN-0086) — governs *transclusion of content*, not two distinct link tuples that happen to carry equal endsets, and must not be invoked to claim more.
>
> (c) *No allocation collision.* Two sub-allocator emissions `K.α`/`K.λ_sh` conflict only when into the same `(d, S)` (H1, H2); clause 2 serializes exactly those, so each reads a distinct frontier (H0) and deposits a fresh, unique address (H2), and clause 5 extends this to whole runs (W2). Writers into distinct `(d, S)` by H1 never collide and need no coordination. Document registration `K.σ` collides only among same-account creations and is covered identically by the account tier of clause 2 (§4/H3).
>
> (d) *No verdict straddles a write.* The case split is by *access count* (§8: V0, V2). A *single-bounded-access* verdict — a single `Observe_K`, an `age` read of one home's frontier `f_d`, an empty-slice `stale` (`N = 0`, one `Observe_K(oper)`), or a single-type `targets_keyed` join (V0) — reads one canonical state by clause 4 (A3, A6, V0), the clause now covering every one-access read rather than `Observe_K` alone. A multi-read verdict — `p ≥ 2` accesses: a cross-type join over two or more types, or a non-empty `stale` enumeration (`N ≥ 1`, single- or multi-home) — under clause 6, has its `p` constituent reads pinned to one committed index `Σ_r` (V2), so it is a statement about one coexisting state and cannot manufacture, in the recognizer's own evaluation, the cross-index error V2's trace exhibits for reads that drift across indices. This is *soundness* about `Σ_r` (V0/V2); *durability* past `r` is V1's separate hypothesis, supplied by no MIC clause. ∎

We can phrase the allocation half as a weakest precondition, which is the most honest summary of clause 2. For an emission into `(d, S)` with the postcondition `R ≡` "the deposited address is fresh and unique,"

`wp(emit into (d,S), R)  ≡  (no other emission into (d,S) is realized between this emission's frontier-read and its deposit).`

The postcondition is *same-home uniqueness alone* — the one property clause 2 actually buys; the gapless `S`-prefix is left out deliberately, since by W1 it is model-intrinsic, kept by the `inc(max,·)` allocator under any interleaving and not driven by the serialization this `wp` isolates. In the totally-ordered model the right-hand side is *automatically true* — there is no "between" two atomic steps to host an interloper. The content of MIC clause 2 is precisely that an implementation, which does not get the total order for free, must *establish* that right-hand side per home. And `wp` makes the scope unmistakable: the precondition quantifies over emissions into *this* `(d, S)` only. It says nothing about other homes — because, by H1, there is nothing to say. **The only thing a concurrent writer must wait for is another writer reaching into the very same sub-allocator; everything else is free.**

## What this note does not cover

- **The scheduler and its fairness.** *Which* agent's proposal is placed at *which* index — the placement that turns a crowd of proposals into the order `𝔼` — is the implementer's and protocol layer's concern. A termination layer may name fairness as a hypothesis; we name it only as the source of `𝔼` and reason about `𝔼` itself, not its construction. No fairness, starvation-freedom, or priority property is asserted here.
- **Agent activation and rule bodies.** What makes an agent fire, and what its firing computes, are opaque — a rule-governance layer leaves rule bodies opaque. We treat a fire only as a batch of operations with linearization points; its internals are not modelled.
- **Inter-server replication / BEBE.** The substrate here is one store. Nelson left the back-end-to-back-end protocol undefined in *Literary Machines* 87.1 ("computer networks are always broken"; each server "at all times unified and operational" but no global agreement). A multi-server consistency model — how per-home orders compose across servers — is a separate note. Our G1 is the natural seam for it (per-home independence is what would let homes live on different servers), but we do not develop it.
- **The concrete concurrency-control mechanism.** MIC is a contract, not an implementation. Whether clause 2 is met by a per-home mutex, an optimistic frontier-CAS with retry, a single global loop (Gregory's choice, which over-satisfies it), or an actor-per-home is unspecified. So is the performance of any of these.
- **Predicate evaluation cost.** The cost of evaluating a verdict's predicate — a predicate-evaluation layer's territory — is not bounded here; we constrain only *when* (one snapshot) it is evaluated, not *how expensively*.

## Open Questions

What is the weakest exclusion primitive that realizes MIC clause 2 — does an optimistic frontier read-and-retry satisfy it without any held lock, and under what condition does retry terminate?

What is the weakest exclusion primitive that realizes MIC clause 6 — can a multi-read verdict be pinned to one committed index by an optimistic read-then-revalidate scheme rather than a held reader-side lock, given the substrate exposes no global version coordinate a reader could cheaply consult?

What is the weakest primitive that realizes MIC clause 7 — can per-coverage-class de-duplication serialization for `idem=⊤` emits be achieved without a global lock, e.g. by a coverage-class-keyed optimistic check-and-deposit with retry, given the dedup-read of `A_K` spans every home and the substrate exposes no per-coverage-class version coordinate?

What is the minimal additional contract that makes a multi-step batch appear atomic *to a reader* — closing the gap A5 isolates so that a reader landing inside a split batch cannot mistake a mid-batch state for a settled one (canonicity, A6, cannot itself distinguish them) — whether by a critical section or a snapshot-readable completion marker, and without reintroducing global serialization?

What relationship between an observer's read index and writers' linearization points must a coordination layer establish to promote a sound quiescence verdict into a durable one, and is that relationship expressible as a substrate-checkable predicate rather than an external assumption?
