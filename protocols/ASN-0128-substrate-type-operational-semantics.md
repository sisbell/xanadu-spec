# ASN-0128: Substrate Type Operational Semantics

*Idem semantics at emit, behavior catalog, default predicates, and standard registrations — the operational layer above ASN-0126's shape framework*

ASN-0086 supplies the typed-relation primitives `Emit_K`, `Observe`, `Nullify` over arity-three tuples. ASN-0126 narrows the framework: single-span sources (`|F| = 1`), three shapes by G span count (Unary `|G| = 0`, Binary `|G| = 1`, Multi `|G|` finite), a static shape-conformance gate applied at every emit via the refined transition relation `→_sh ≡ K.σ ∪ K.α ∪ K.λ_sh`, and an immutable registry keyed by coverage class whose value is the type's shape — and the shape alone. What ASN-0126 deliberately defers to a successor — its Open Questions 1–4 — is the subject of this note.

**Depends:** ASN-0043 (Link Ontology), ASN-0086 (Typed Relations on Address Sets), ASN-0126 (Substrate Shape Framework).

## What this note commits

- **Registration record.** Extends the registry value from the shape alone (ASN-0126, The registry) to the triple `(shape, idem, behaviors)`, with a well-formedness condition and invariance inherited from ASN-0126's write discipline.
- **Idem operational semantics.** Specifies what the surface operation `Emit_K` does when the emitted tuple is "the same" as an existing active tuple of a type this registry marks idempotent — the criterion of sameness (coverage equality), a full hit/miss contract consolidated with its caller-facing weakest precondition (I6), interaction with nullification, born-nullified emits, and concurrent writers. Answers ASN-0126 Open Question 1, whose "successor registry" is the registration record above.
- **Behavior catalog.** Four substrate-provided behaviors apps optionally attach at registration: read-filter, determinate-walk, typed-reverse-lookup, age-staleness. Each unlocks specific predicates whose semantics this note pins. Answers ASN-0126 Open Question 2.
- **Default predicates.** Three predicates every registered type receives, derived from ASN-0086's `Observe_K`. Answers ASN-0126 Open Question 3.
- **Standard registrations.** Three coverage classes the substrate pre-ships in `Σ_init.registry` — the retraction relation R and two universal lifecycle markers — settling ASN-0126 Open Question 4 (which left open whether anything ships pre-registered). R's registration policy routes all retraction through the unit-depth wrapper `Nullify_Binary` and preconditions P-tgt, turning the P-tgt-*conditional* postconditions of ASN-0126's Nullify_Binary contract into guarantees and settling Open Question 7.

## The registration record

ASN-0126's registry stores, per coverage-class key, the type's shape and nothing else; its dynamics never write the registry after `Σ_init` (P1, RegistryInvariance). This note extends the *value* of each entry from `shape` to the record

`(shape, idem, behaviors)`

where `idem ∈ {⊤, ⊥}` and `behaviors` is a finite set drawn from the catalog below. The keys — coverage classes, unique under `~` — are unchanged.

**R-C0 (RecordWellFormedness).** A registration record is well-formed when `shape ∈ {Unary, Binary, Multi}`, `idem ∈ {⊤, ⊥}`, and every attached behavior is compatible with the rest of the record: read-filter (BH1) requires Unary; determinate-walk (BH2) and typed-reverse-lookup (BH3) require Binary; age-staleness (BH4) requires `idem = ⊥`, with no shape clause. R-C0 is enforced by failing construction (R-VAL). `Σ_init.registry` is well-formed entry-wise, on top of ASN-0126's C0 (finiteness, key uniqueness, representatives in `T_admissible`).

**R-VAL (ConstructionValidation).** Both well-formedness conditions are decidable at `Σ_init` construction, and construction is where the substrate checks them — the registry's only write point (ASN-0126, The registry). C0's key uniqueness is pairwise `CoverageEqualityDecidable` (ASN-0086) over the finitely many stored representatives — `O(|registry|²)` decidable tests, each on the representative endsets directly; membership of each representative in `T_admissible` is non-emptiness of a finite span set; and R-C0's clauses are finite case checks per entry (shape and idem against their enumerations, each attached behavior against the compatibility table). A declaration set failing any test yields no `Σ_init`: there is no partially-registered substrate, and no runtime path ever re-validates. What a passing declaration set yields is constructed exactly as ASN-0126 constructs its own initial state (its ForgetfulProjection): `Σ_init` is the validated extended-record registry adjoined to ASN-0086's three initial components, altering none of them — `Σ_init.C`, `Σ_init.M`, `Σ_init.L` are `Σ_init^{0086}`'s components verbatim, so in particular `Σ_init.L = ∅`.

**R-TR (ExtendedRecordTransition).** Over extended-record states the transition relation is

`→_sh ≡ K.σ ∪ K.α ∪ K.λ_sh`

with ASN-0126's step effects and preconditions throughout (its GatedTransitionRelation), the registry clauses read against the extended record: `K.λ_sh`'s precondition (0) — the deposited value is a standard triple — is unchanged; (i) "K is registered" is a key-side test, and the keys are unchanged; (ii) `Sh-conf(K, F, G)` reads the stored record's shape component, the only part of the value `Sh-conf` consults (ASN-0126, Shape-conformance); and each step kind's registry frame `Σ'.registry = Σ.registry` ranges over the triple-valued registry. `→_sh*` is the reflexive-transitive closure, and *reachable* means `→_sh*`-reachable from R-VAL's `Σ_init`, as in ASN-0126's Definition Reachability.

**R1 (ExtendedRegistryInvariance).** The extended record is constant at every `→_sh*`-reachable state (R-TR). ASN-0126's P1 argument is frame-based — every step kind carries `Σ'.registry = Σ.registry` in its frame (R-TR) and no step has the registry in its effect — and reads no part of the stored value, so it lifts to the extended value verbatim.

**R2 (IdemStability).** For any registered K, `idem(K)` takes the same value at every reachable state — immediate from R1, exactly as ASN-0126's P2 (ShapeStability) follows for the shape component.

The gate's verdicts are untouched by the extension (R-TR), but the state space changed — registry values are now triples, not shapes — so ASN-0126's results do not apply verbatim; we cross the change as ASN-0126 crosses its own, with a projection and transfer clauses mirroring its π, ProjectionBridge, and B1–B3.

**RP (RecordProjection).** Let `ρ` act on states by keeping C, M, L and every registry key, and projecting each registry value `(shape, idem, behaviors) ↦ shape`. Then: (i) `ρ(Σ_init)` is a well-formed ASN-0126 initial state — its C, M, L are ASN-0086's initial components by `Σ_init`'s construction (R-VAL), exactly what ASN-0126 adjoins its registry to; C0's clauses (finiteness, key uniqueness, representatives in `T_admissible`) are key-side and untouched; and every projected value is a bare shape. (ii) Each `→_sh` step over extended-record states (R-TR) maps under `ρ` to an ASN-0126 `→_sh` step: the gate's verdict agrees, because `Sh-conf` reads `(F, G)` and the shape component, which `ρ` preserves; the C/M/L effects are identical; and each step kind's registry frame `Σ'.registry = Σ.registry` projects to the same frame. (iii) By induction on derivation length, every reachable extended-record state `Σ` has `ρ(Σ)` ASN-0126-reachable.

*RP-a (single-state transfer).* An ASN-0126 claim whose conclusion is a predicate of one reachable state, reading only C, M, L, registration status (key-side, `ρ`-preserved), and shapes, holds at every reachable extended-record state Σ by evaluation at `ρ(Σ)`, ASN-0126-reachable by (iii) and sharing the read components.

*RP-b (path transfer).* A claim whose conclusion constrains *steps or successors* is not a predicate of one state, and evaluation at `ρ(Σ)` alone cannot deliver it; it transfers by derivation projection instead. By (ii) and induction, an extended-record derivation `Σ →_sh* Θ` projects to an ASN-0126 derivation `ρ(Σ) →_sh* ρ(Θ)`; single-state hypotheses hold at `ρ(Σ)` by sharing; the ASN-0126 claim applied there constrains `ρ(Θ)`; and the per-successor conclusion, again over shared components, pulls back to Θ. A transition invariant transfers the same way across each genuine extended-record step: the step's projection is the step it quantifies over.

*RP-c (step lift).* Conversely, an ASN-0126 claim asserting the *existence* of a `→_sh` step lifts to the extended-record system when the step's preconditions read only `ρ`-preserved data: the preconditions hold at Σ iff at `ρ(Σ)`, the C/M/L effects are determined identically, and every step kind frames the registry — the asserted step is an extended-record step verbatim. P5 (GateRealizability) is the canonical instance.

## Denotation and views

The operation surface this note exposes presents *address sets*, and the behavior and default predicates below return *addresses*; both read the store through a *view*.

**AD (AddressDenotation).** Address-typed results cannot be coverages: a single unit-depth span `(x, δ(1, #x))` covers the entire subtree `{t : x ≼ t}` (PrefixSpanCoverage, ASN-0043) — an infinite set — so "return the coverage" is not an operation, and a Binary tuple's one G-span does not by itself name one address. We bridge with a convention the corpus already half-owns: ASN-0043's L13 designates the unit-depth span at `b` as *the canonical span* for referencing the entity at `b`. Read in reverse: a span is *address-denoting* iff it is unit-depth, `(x, δ(1, #x))`, and it *denotes* its start `x`. An endset `e` is address-denoting iff every span in it is, and its denoted set is `addrs(e) = {x : (x, δ(1, #x)) ∈ e}` — finite, since endsets are finite span sets (`Endset = 𝒫_fin(Span)`, ASN-0043), and read off the spans by inspection. The convention runs forward as well: for a finite address set `X ⊆ T`, the *canonical encoding* is `enc(X) = {(x, δ(1, #x)) : x ∈ X}` — one canonical span per address, each T12-well-formed (`#x ≥ 1` by T0) — and `addrs(enc(X)) = X`. The operation surface speaks addresses through it: the exposed `Emit_K` emits canonical encodings (the exposed signature, Idem operational semantics), and `Nullify_Binary`'s fixed endsets are encodings of singletons — its from-fill `(d_retr, δ(1, #d_retr))` and its to-span `(a, δ(1, #a))` are both unit-depth — so every endset the surface emits is address-denoting by construction.

Two result regimes follow. *Membership predicates* (`is_K`, `is_filtered`) take an address argument and test coverage — `addr ∈ coverage(F)` — total over all tuples, address-denoting or not, and decidable: per-span membership is a T2 comparison pair, endsets are finite, and L-fin bounds the tuples. Under AD, coverage membership is subtree membership: the tested address is a denoted address *or one of its extensions*, exactly L13's reading. *Enumeration predicates* (`members`, `targets_of`, `sources_to`, `succs`, `tip`, `chain`, `target_of`, `targets_keyed`) return denoted addresses, assembled from `addrs(·)` of the relevant endsets — each result finite and computable (L-fin many tuples, finitely many spans each). `is_in_chain` is Boolean-valued but *enumeration-derived*, not a membership predicate; its definition is BH2's.

*Verdict for nonconforming tuples.* A tuple whose relevant endset carries a non-unit-depth span is reachable only off-surface (raw `K.λ_sh`). Such a tuple contributes to enumeration results only the denotations of its address-denoting spans; a non-denoting span singles out no address, so an address-typed enumeration honestly omits it. The tuple remains fully visible to the membership predicates (which read coverage) and to raw `Observe_K`.

**AM (ArgumentMatching).** The regimes fix what the enumeration family *returns*; how its predicates match their address *arguments* is a separate rule, and one choice does not fit both slots. An argument naming a **source vertex** — `targets_of(x)`, `succs(x)`, `chain(addr)`, `tip(addr)`, `is_in_chain(addr, ·)`, `target_of(source, K)`, `targets_keyed(source)` — is matched by *exact denotation*: the tuples consulted are those whose F denotes the argument, `x ∈ addrs(F)`. The family's one reverse lookup, whose argument names an **asserted-about address** — `sources_to(target)` — is matched by *coverage*: `target ∈ coverage(G)`. (`members` takes no address argument; BH4's `age` takes an exact chain address and `stale` an ordinal horizon `h ∈ ℕ` — neither matches an endset.) The asymmetry is chosen, and the implementation is not what chooses it: Gregory's link discovery matches the from-side and the to-side *identically* — one search path, parameterized only by which endset dimension it reads, each side decided by non-empty I-span intersection, partial overlap sufficing, the two sides then intersected by link identity; neither side is exact-matched. Coverage matching is thus the substrate's native query semantics on both slots, and the note keeps it wherever the question is the native one: `Observe_K` patterns, the membership predicates, and `sources_to`, whose question — which sources assert *about* this address — is the membership question relativized to the G-slot, inheriting the membership regime; coverage-keying there also reads through range-valued to-coverages, which the substrate itself produces (retraction subtrees) and off-surface tuples may carry. The forward family is denotation-keyed for the opposite reason: its arguments are not asserted-about addresses but *vertices of the denoted graph* (BH2), and the walk machinery is sound only on exact vertices — `chain`'s distinctness check, cycle detection, and termination bound all compare addresses by equality, so a coverage-keyed `succs` would let `chain(t.x)` walk edges asserted at `t` while the equality-based cycle check missed coverage-level revisits: the determinate walk would not be determinate on its own terms. The assertion-level forward reading this refuses on the F-slot is recovered at D3 (`targets_under`).

**Views.** Three result-set lenses recur below; the first two are ASN-0086's `Observe_K` selectors under new names:

- the **audit view** of K at Σ is the audit slice `L_K^Σ` (ASN-0086's `hist`) — every K-tuple ever deposited, nullified or not;
- the **active view** is the active subset `A_K^Σ` (ASN-0086's `oper`) — deposited and not nullified;
- the **default view** is the active view after the read-filter rewrite — mechanics, coincidence cases included, in BH1's Rewrite scope.

The default predicates D1–D3 are *defined* on the active view; the audit view is reached only through `Observe_K(…, hist)`.

**View selection.** The two enumeration surfaces the read-filter rewrites — `members` and `targets_of` (BH1's Rewrite scope) — carry a view selector: `members(K', view)` and `targets_of(x, view)` with `view ∈ {default, active}`, mirroring `Observe_K`'s `View ∈ {hist, oper}`; a call that omits the selector reads `default`. The selector chooses between the surface's two committed readings — under `active`, the equations D1/D3; under `default`, their BH1 rewrites. No other surface carries the selector: the remaining enumeration predicates and the membership predicates each have a single committed reading, the active view.

The escape hatches are asymmetric: filtering is undone by selecting `active`; nullification is undone by nothing (R6c), only resurfaced in the audit view.

## Idem operational semantics

What does `idem(K)` commit the emit surface to — and what does "the same tuple" even mean? The registration record carries the flag (above); here is its semantics. Throughout, `F, G` name a surface call's emitted endsets (canonical encodings — the exposed signature, below); stored incumbents, raw deposits included, range over arbitrary endsets.

**I0 (SamenessIsCoverageEquality).** Two emitted pairs `(F, G)` and `(F', G')` are *the same* for de-duplication iff `coverage(F) = coverage(F')` and `coverage(G) = coverage(G')` — `coverage` over endsets `Endset = 𝒫_fin(Span)` as ASN-0043 defines it — each equality decidable by CoverageEqualityDecidable (ASN-0086). Coverage is what a tuple *asserts* — a denoted address asserts its entire subtree, so listing an extension alongside its own root adds nothing to the assertion — and sameness for de-duplication is sameness of assertion. A strictly finer criterion is available — denoted-set equality, `addrs(F) = addrs(F') ∧ addrs(G) = addrs(G')` (`addrs` per AD; strictly finer by I0a's separating pair) — but under it the active subset could hold coverage-equal tuples that no argument selects between. The matching surfaces are immediate: the membership predicates, `Observe` patterns, and `same_type` all test coverage, never decomposition (TypeEquivalence, ASN-0086). The denotation-keyed surfaces — AM's forward family, which consults tuples by `x ∈ addrs(F)` — close by cases on the slot a separating pair could inhabit. Not F: every stored tuple has `|F| = 1` (Sh-conf at every shape — ASN-0126's P6, at extended-record states by RP-a), and a single span is determined by its coverage — the start is the coverage's T1-least element; equal half-open intervals force equal endpoints `s ⊕ ℓ = s ⊕ ℓ'` (the lesser of two unequal endpoints would lie in one interval and not the other); and the shared start cancels from the equal sums by TA-LC (LeftCancellation, ASN-0034), whose preconditions are exactly the spans' T12 well-formedness — each displacement positive with action point at most `#s` (`Endset = 𝒫_fin(Span)`, ASN-0043) — giving `ℓ = ℓ'`: coverage-equal F-slots are the *same* endset and every F-keyed argument consults both members of a pair identically. Not a Unary or Binary G: vacuously (`G = ∅`) or by the same single-span identity (`|G| = 1`). The one slot left is a Multi G, and there decomposition is read only in *result* position — `targets_of` unions `addrs(G)` over all F-matching tuples (D3) — never matched against an argument (the family's one G-keyed argument match, `sources_to`, is coverage-keyed by AM, and Binary-scoped besides): no argument selects one member of the pair over the other. The conclusion the cases support is argument-blindness, not invisibility: coverage-equal tuples' differing decompositions do surface in *result* position — `Observe_K` returns stored triples whole, and the enumeration unions reflect stored denoted sets — but the matching surfaces are blind to them and no argument steers a query between the pair's members. We reject the finer criterion on that ground.

**I0a (MinimalElementsIdentity).** For an address-denoting endset `e`, the ≼-minimal elements of `coverage(e)` are exactly the ≼-minimal elements of `addrs(e)`. The *separating pair* fixes what is at stake: an endset holding unit-depth spans at `t` and at an extension `t.x` denotes `{t, t.x}`, while one holding the span at `t` alone denotes `{t}` — the denoted sets differ, the coverages are equal by subtree absorption (`subtree(t.x) ⊆ subtree(t)`, PrefixSpanCoverage). The pair also witnesses the strictness I0 claims for the refinement: equal denotations force equal coverages — an address-denoting endset's coverage is the union of its denoted subtrees (PrefixSpanCoverage) — but not conversely.

(⊆) Let `t` be ≼-minimal in `coverage(e)`. The coverage is the union of the denoted subtrees, so `r ≼ t` for some denoted `r`; `r` itself lies in the coverage (reflexivity of ≼ puts each denoted address in its own subtree), so `t`'s minimality forces `t = r` — `t` is denoted. And `t` is minimal among denoted addresses: a denoted `r' ≺ t` would lie in the coverage and contradict `t`'s minimality there.

(⊇) Let `r` be ≼-minimal among denoted addresses. Then `r` lies in the coverage (reflexivity again), and is minimal there: a coverage element `t ≺ r` would lie in some denoted `r''`'s subtree, giving a denoted `r'' ≼ t ≺ r` and contradicting `r`'s minimality among denoted addresses.

The coverage is in turn recovered from those minimal listings: `addrs(e)` is finite (`Endset = 𝒫_fin(Span)`), so every denoted address extends some ≼-minimal one and its subtree is absorbed into that one's. An address-denoting endset's coverage thus determines and is determined by its ≼-minimal denoted addresses.

**I1 (IdemDedupSemantics).** Under `idem(K) = ⊤`, the de-duplication check belongs to the *surface operation* `Emit_K(Σ, d, F, G)` and to it alone; the transition relation is R-TR's `→_sh`, unchanged — the idem flag enters no step's precondition or effect. The contract has four clauses.

*Order — gate first.* The gate's preconditions (K registered and `Sh-conf(K, F, G)`; arity 3 — ASN-0126's precondition (0) — holds by construction, the surface assembling the triple itself) are evaluated on the presented values before any dedup consultation; a gate-failing call is rejected — no step, no address — even when an I0-equal active tuple exists. The order is observable: a two-span F rejected by a `|F| = 1` shape can be I0-equal to an active single-span F'; gate-first rejects that call rather than answering it with the existing address.

*Miss.* If no member of `A_K^Σ` is I0-equal to `(F, G)`, `Emit_K` invokes `K.λ_sh`, and ASN-0086's emit contract holds as inherited through the gate: a fresh `a = a_emit(Σ, d)`, `home(a) = d`, the deposit `(F, G, K)` at `a`, frame on C, M, and registry.

*Hit.* If some `(a', F', G') ∈ A_K^Σ` is I0-equal to `(F, G)`, `Emit_K` returns `a'` and **takes no step**: `Σ' = Σ`, nothing is deposited, and ASN-0086's emit postconditions are forfeited wholesale — `dom(Σ.L)` does not grow, no address is fresh, and the returned address's home is `home(a')`, which may differ from the caller's `d`. The `d` argument is consulted only on a miss; cross-home de-duplication is deliberate, because I0's identity is `(coverage(F), coverage(G))` within `[K]` and nothing else — proposer, emission time, source app, home document, *and span decomposition* are all outside it. In particular, a hit can fire on a call whose *denoted* sets differ from the incumbent's (the separating pair, I0a) — and the suppressed call's decomposition is then never stored. Every enumeration predicate thereafter reflects the incumbent tuple's denoted sets, never the suppressed call's: on the pair, `targets_of` answers the incumbent's denoted set, the explicitly presented extension never enumerated (AD). The matching surfaces lose nothing, the two coverages being equal by hypothesis; and the loss is avoidable at the source: where emitters present coverage-irredundant address lists — no listed address extending another — denoted-set equality and coverage equality coincide on the presented lists — an irredundant address list is a ≼-antichain, hence equal to its own ≼-minimal set, which by I0a is the ≼-minimal set of its coverage, so coverage-equal endsets carry equal presented lists — and a hit suppresses no denotation at all. Along a K-surface-emitted derivation, the matching tuple is unique (I1a); off that discipline several may match, and `Emit_K` returns the T1-least matching address — well-defined, since the matches form a finite non-empty subset of `dom(Σ.L)` (L-fin, ASN-0043) and T1 orders it totally.

*Home validation — branch-local.* ASN-0086's `Emit_K` carries the precondition `d ∈ dom(Σ.M)`, and `a_emit` is total only there (EmitAddress, ASN-0086); this surface checks the condition exactly where `d` is read. On a miss — the only branch that reads `d` — the check is enforced by rejection: a miss with `d ∉ dom(Σ.M)` takes no step and returns no address, exactly as a gate failure does (a deposit homed at an unallocated `d` would violate L1a, LinkScopedAllocation, ASN-0043 — the relation has no such step). On a hit, `d` is not read at all: the call is admitted and answered with the existing address whatever `d` holds, `d ∉ dom(Σ.M)` included. Admission of an invalid-`d` call is therefore state-dependent — it turns on whether a hit exists, which the caller in general cannot evaluate — so the portable contract is to supply `d ∈ dom(Σ.M)` unconditionally, under which the branch distinction never surfaces in admission (validate-where-read is Gregory's discipline as well: the document check sits on the execution path that actually opens the document).

The check's rules are state-independent (they read the input values and the registry, invariant by R1); its *result* depends on the active subset, which varies across states. By R2, the same rules apply to every K-emit at every reachable state. The relation being unchanged, P5 (GateRealizability) survives (RP). A dedup hit creates nothing and merges nothing — `idem = ⊤` is a *computed* equivalence at the operation surface, the substrate beneath it pluralistic (Nelson's architecture likewise: links are never merged, equivalence among them computed at query time, never stored as identity).

**I1a (ActiveIdemUniqueness).** Deposit history is a property of *derivations*, not states: a raw `K.λ_sh` step depositing the value an `Emit_K` miss would deposit lands at the same frontier address (FrontierUnification, ASN-0126, by RP-a) and yields the identical post-state, so a state does not determine how its tuples arrived. The hypothesis is therefore a step classification, in the idiom the foundation already owns — ASN-0086's RelationalLayer classifies the `L_R`-growing `→`-steps, and its LayerReachable quantifies over states reached by conforming step sequences: a derivation `Σ_init →_sh* Σ` is *K-surface-emitted* iff every `L_K`-growing step along it — every `Θ →_sh Θ'` with `L_K^Θ ⊊ L_K^{Θ'}` — is the deposit branch of an `Emit_K` invocation (no raw `K.λ_sh` bypass; for [R], the wrapper routes through `Emit_R`, so wrapper-routed steps qualify). For `idem(K) = ⊤`, the active subset holds at most one tuple per I0-class at every state a K-surface-emitted derivation reaches:

`(A (a, F, G), (a', F', G') ∈ A_K^Σ : coverage(F) = coverage(F') ∧ coverage(G) = coverage(G') : a = a')`

*Proof* by induction along the K-surface-emitted derivation. Base: `L_K^{Σ_init} = ∅`, vacuous. Step: K.σ and K.α leave `Σ.L` unchanged. A `K.λ_sh` deposit of a non-K tuple leaves `L_K` unchanged and can only *shrink* `A_K` (a retraction deposit may nullify a K-address; nothing re-activates — R6c via ASN-0126's B3 and RP-b). A `K.λ_sh` deposit of a K-tuple grows `L_K`, hence is by the derivation's classification the deposit branch of an `Emit_K` — which fires only on a miss: at the pre-state its I0-class had no active member, so at the post-state it has at most one — the deposit itself, when it lands active rather than born nullified. No tuple changes class post hoc — coverage is a pure function of the stored endsets, which are immutable (L12 via ASN-0126's B2 and RP-b). The deposit's effect on every *other* I0-class splits on K's relation to [R]. For K ≁ R the deposit is no retraction tuple and nullifies nothing: every other class's active membership is untouched. For K ~ R — the wrapper-routed instantiation the hypothesis admits — the deposit may nullify a member of another class: a wrapper deposit targeting an R-tuple address (retraction-of-retraction, contemplated outright by R6b) removes that class's tuple from active membership. But nullification only *shrinks* a class — the same observation made for non-K deposits above — and a class holding at most one active member holds at most one after removal. ∎

**I2 (AuditSliceNotConsulted).** The de-duplication test reads `A_K^Σ`, not the audit slice `L_K^Σ`. A tuple emitted and later nullified is in `L_K^Σ` but not in `A_K^Σ`; a subsequent `Emit_K` with the same `(F, G)` is **not** a no-op — it attempts a fresh tuple at a new address. The audit trail records both the original (now nullified) and the new emissions; resurrection-after-nullification is the design intent, not a side effect — it is exactly the restoration route ASN-0086 already names (R6c, RestorationByReemission: a fresh tuple at a fresh address). One caveat bounds it: the fresh address is the frontier slot of the home's link chain — `a_emit(Σ, d) = chain_d(f_d^Σ)`, a predicate of the one state Σ (FrontierUnification, ASN-0126), holding at extended-record states by RP-a — and if a prior range-G retraction's coverage includes that slot, the resurrection emit is itself born nullified — irreversibly, by Corollary RangeSterilization (ASN-0126; successor-quantified, so it crosses to extended-record states by RP-b's derivation projection, not by RP-a). Resurrection is guaranteed only where the home chain's next slots are unsterilized.

**I3 (BornNullifiedTransparency).** ASN-0126's wp analysis (Weakest precondition of the shape-gated emit) separates the gate from the landing: a gate-clearing emit deposits into the audit slice `L_K^{Σ'}`, but lands in the active subset `A_K^{Σ'}` only if two further conjuncts hold — C2 (the emit is not a self-nullifying retraction) and C3 (no pre-existing retraction tuple's coverage includes the fresh address). Under either failure the tuple is *born nullified*: in `L_K^{Σ'}`, absent from `A_K^{Σ'}`. By I2, a later `idem = ⊤` emit with the same `(F, G)` does not see a born-nullified tuple in its dedup check; it consults only the active subset, from which the born-nullified tuple is absent — whether the later emit misses outright turns on the rest of the class, since an intervening same-pair deposit may have landed active in the interim.

**SD (SurfaceDiscipline).** I1a's idiom instantiated at [R]: a derivation `Σ_init →_sh* Σ` is *surface-disciplined* iff every `L_R`-growing step along it — every `Θ →_sh Θ'` with `L_R^Θ ⊊ L_R^{Θ'}` — is a `Nullify_Binary` invocation. This is ASN-0086's RelationalLayer commitment ("every `→`-step with `L_R^Σ ⊊ L_R^{Σ'}` is a `Nullify`") read against `→_sh` and this note's wrapper; the results below quantify over the states surface-disciplined derivations reach — the analog of ASN-0086's LayerReachable. Since the exposed `Emit_K` rejects every R-class call (the `K ≁ R` precondition, I6), the wrapper is the only surface route into `L_R`, so a derivation is surface-disciplined exactly when it is [R]-surface-emitted (I1a).

**DR (DisciplineRestoration — statement).** Along a surface-disciplined derivation, no retraction tuple's to-coverage ever contains a later emit's fresh address: C3's existential is empty over `L_R^Θ` at every state Θ the derivation reaches, so wp conjunct C3 holds at every gate-clearing emit, a tuple is born nullified only through C2's self-nullification, and sterilization (Corollary RangeSterilization, ASN-0126) is unreachable. Proof and wrapper wp: Standard registrations.

**I4 (ConcurrentEmitFirstCommit).** Two writers emitting tuples with same `(F, G, K)` against the same Σ race *ahead of* the substrate relation: `→_sh` is a sequential, interleaved step relation (it inherits ASN-0086's model — concurrency has no semantics inside it), so a serializing authority orders the two calls before either becomes a step. The emission address is pinned per home to the chain frontier (FrontierUnification, ASN-0126), so allocation is first-to-commit. For `idem = ⊤`: the winner deposits at the fresh address, and *provided the deposit lands active* (I3's caveat), the loser's emit, evaluated against the winner's post-state, finds the now-active tuple by I1 and returns the winner's address. If the winner's deposit is born nullified, the loser's dedup check sees an empty class (I2, I3) and the loser deposits a second tuple at the next frontier slot — itself subject to I3. For `idem = ⊥`: both emissions produce distinct addresses, and both tuples appear in `A_K^{Σ₂}`, the second writer's post-state — again modulo I3's born-nullified cases. Where both calls extend a surface-disciplined derivation (SD), C3 cannot fail at either (DR) and C2 concerns only self-nullifying retraction emits, so for non-R types every deposit lands active and the unqualified first-to-commit reading is exact.

**I5 (IdemFalseAlwaysFresh).** Under `idem(K) = ⊥`, no de-duplication test runs, and home validation is unconditional: with no hit branch, every call reads `d`, and a call with `d ∉ dom(Σ.M)` is rejected — no step, no address — exactly as I1's miss branch rejects, on I1's ground. Every gate-clearing call with `d ∈ dom(Σ.M)` invokes `K.λ_sh` and produces a fresh address regardless of `(F, G)` content, and the new tuple appears in `A_K^{Σ'}` (modulo I3's born-nullified cases).

*The exposed signature.* ASN-0086 declares `Emit_K : Σ × dom(Σ.M) × Endset × Endset → Σ' × A_rel^{Σ'}`; the surface this note exposes narrows the content slots to finite address sets, widens the home slot, and admits rejection:

`Emit_K : Σ × T × ℘_fin(T) × ℘_fin(T) ⇀ Σ' × A_rel^{Σ'}`

— a partial operation presenting `(d, X_F, X_G)`, whose emitted endsets are the canonical encodings `F = enc(X_F)`, `G = enc(X_G)` (AD). The standard triple `(F, G, K)` is thus assembled by the surface itself: arity 3 holds by construction and contributes no checkable precondition, exactly as ASN-0126's WP discharges its precondition (0). The home slot is `T` rather than `dom(Σ.M)` because home validation is branch-local (I1): the signature cannot pre-restrict a slot some admitted calls never read. The partiality is rejection — where a call is rejected, no step fires and no address is returned, so the operation is undefined there rather than total with an error value. The outcome taxonomy — rejection, hit, deposit — and its per-branch contract are I6's, with I5's idem-⊥ branch as its corollary.

**I6 (IdemEmitSurfaceContract).** The caller-facing contract for `Emit_K` under `idem(K) = ⊤`, with its weakest precondition. ASN-0086's wp Case 2 and ASN-0126's WP are computed for the raw, always-stepping gated emit; under `idem = ⊤` the surface may take no step, and on a hit the returned address bears the incumbent's stored `(F', G')`, not the presented `(F, G)` — so the inherited postcondition `(a★, F, G) ∈ A_K^{Σ'}` is in general *false* at the returned address: the address carries exactly one triple (SliceUniqueness, ASN-0086), the incumbent's, and I0 admits hits whose stored decomposition differs from the presented one (the separating pair, I0a). The caller-facing postcondition is coverage-typed instead, exactly what I0 makes the call's assertion:

`POST(a★) ≡ (E F★, G★ :: (a★, F★, G★) ∈ A_K^{Σ'} ∧ coverage(F★) = coverage(F) ∧ coverage(G★) = coverage(G))`

— the returned address bears an active K-tuple asserting what the call asserted.

*Preconditions — uniform.* Three clauses on the presented values, checked first on every call (I1's order): the gate — K registered and `Sh-conf(K, F, G)`; arity 3, ASN-0126's precondition (0), holds by construction at this surface and contributes no clause (the exposed signature) — and the retraction exclusion `K ≁ R`, decided by one `CoverageEqualityDecidable` test of the presented endset against the shipped representative `K_R` (S3). A call failing any clause is rejected, no step, no address. (The wrapper's internal `Emit_R` is not a surface call; `Nullify_Binary` is the one retraction entry point, S3.)

*Branch condition.* The admitted call is a *hit* iff some `(a', F', G') ∈ A_K^Σ` is I0-equal to the presented pair, a *miss* otherwise (I1).

*Hit.* No step: `Σ' = Σ`; returned address `a★ = a'`, the T1-least match (unique along K-surface-emitted derivations, I1a). POST holds at `Σ' = Σ` from the incumbent itself: `(a', F', G') ∈ A_K^Σ` with both coverages equal by the branch condition. The branch reads no further input — `d` in particular is not consulted (I1) — so it contributes no conjunct beyond the gate: a hit establishes POST unconditionally.

*Miss.* The branch reads `d` and rejects `d ∉ dom(Σ.M)` (I1's home validation: no step, no address), contributing `d ∈ dom(Σ.M)`. An admitted miss takes the `K.λ_sh` step (RP-c): fresh `a★ = a_emit(Σ, d)`, deposit `(F, G, K)` at `a★`, frame on C, M, and registry. POST at the post-state is then `(a★, F, G) ∈ A_K^{Σ'}` — coverages equal reflexively — which is exactly the landing ASN-0126's WP analyzes: it requires C2 (`K ≁ R ∨ a_emit(Σ, d) ∉ coverage(G)`) and C3 (no pre-existing retraction tuple's to-coverage contains `a_emit(Σ, d)`). C2's first disjunct is the surface's own `K ≁ R` precondition, so C2 holds at every admitted call; what remains live is C3, under whose failure the deposit is born nullified (I3) and POST fails.

*The wp, assembled.* Writing `pre` for the three uniform preconditions and `hit(Σ, F, G)` for the branch condition:

`wp(Emit_K under idem = ⊤, POST) ≡ pre ∧ (hit(Σ, F, G) ∨ (d ∈ dom(Σ.M) ∧ C3))`

(C2 is absorbed into `pre`: its first disjunct is the `K ≁ R` clause.) The equivalence is read as ASN-0126's WP lemma reads its own wp — the attainability convention `wp(g → S, R) ≡ g ∧ wp(S, R)` is in force: on a rejected call nothing fires and the wp is false outright. The convention is load-bearing for necessity: POST is parameterized by the returned address, and a rejected call returns none, so on rejection POST has no instance to be false at — indeed an active tuple satisfying POST's body may stand while the call is rejected (I1's gate-first order). Necessity therefore splits by route: a rejected call — a `pre` failure, or a miss with invalid `d` — falsifies the wp by the convention alone, no step firing; an admitted miss failing C3 falsifies POST genuinely at the returned address — `a★ = a_emit(Σ, d)` is fresh, so the born-nullified deposit (I3) is the only tuple at `a★`, absent from `A_K^{Σ'}`, and POST's existential has no witness. Sufficiency is the per-branch contract above: a hit establishes POST from the incumbent, and an admitted miss satisfying C3 (C2 holding from `pre`) lands its deposit active.

*Disciplined-domain reduction.* At states reached by surface-disciplined derivations (SD) — and every derivation driven through this surface alone is one, the surface rejecting raw R-class calls (S3) — the C3 conjunct vanishes (DR). For the surface this note actually exposes, then:

`wp(Emit_K, POST) ≡ pre ∧ (hit(Σ, F, G) ∨ d ∈ dom(Σ.M))`

— the uniform preconditions, and either an active I0-duplicate (which answers the call without reading `d`) or a valid home.

*Corollary — the idem-⊥ wp.* I5's branch is this contract's miss with `hit ≡ ⊥`: the same uniform preconditions, the same `d`-validation, the same `K.λ_sh` step on admission — and the same POST measures it exactly, because POST is evaluated at the *returned* address: a C3 failure falsifies it outright, the returned `a★` carrying only the born-nullified deposit, whatever I0-equal tuples stand active at other addresses (under `idem = ⊥` duplicates accumulate, I5, but POST never consults them). Setting `hit ≡ ⊥` in the assembled wp:

`wp(Emit_K under idem = ⊥, POST) ≡ pre ∧ d ∈ dom(Σ.M) ∧ C3`

(C2 absorbed into `pre` as before), reducing at states reached by surface-disciplined derivations to `pre ∧ d ∈ dom(Σ.M)` exactly as the disciplined-domain reduction above.

## Behaviors

A behavior is substrate-provided machinery an app optionally attaches at registration, as the `behaviors` component of the registration record; attaching one unlocks additional predicates. Behaviors compose; an app may attach any subset compatible with the registered shape and idem flag (R-C0).

### BH1 (read-filter)

**Applies to:** Unary
**Effect:** Addresses lying in the F-coverage of an active tuple of this type are excluded from the *result sets* of the default view's two enumeration surfaces on every other registered type (Rewrite scope below; views in Denotation and views). The exclusion is *coverage-scoped* — the rewrite drops a result element through `is_filtered`, a membership predicate (Provides, below) — so for a surface-emitted mark, whose F is address-denoting (AD), the dropped set is the marked address *and every extension of it*: AD's assertion doctrine applied to the filter mark, a denoted address asserting its entire subtree. The subtree reach is intended, not an artifact of the regime: a Unary mark at a document-level address filters the document's whole subtree from default enumerations — Nelson's lifecycle marks are container-level metalinks whose scope embraces everything stored beneath the marked address ("links may be made to them which embrace all the contents below them" [LM 4/23]), routing default presentation away from the marked entity's internal parts while destroying none of them.
**Provides:** `is_filtered(addr) → Bool` — true iff some `(a, F, ∅) ∈ A_K^Σ` has `addr ∈ coverage(F)`: a membership predicate on the filter type's own active view, total and decidable (AD).
**Rewrite scope.** BH1 rewrites exactly two enumeration surfaces, on every registered `K' ≠ K`, and the rewrites of all read-filter registrations compose by union: writing `Φ` for the set of types registered with BH1, each contributing its own `is_filtered_J` over its own active view, the default view `members(K', default)` is `{x ∈ members(K', active) : (A J ∈ Φ, J ≠ K' : ¬is_filtered_J(x))}` — equivalently, the union of the BH1 types' filtered sets is subtracted — and `targets_of(x, default)` likewise drops the denoted targets so filtered; with a single BH1 registration this reads `{x ∈ members(K', active) : ¬is_filtered(x)}`. The two views coincide where the subtraction removes nothing — definitionally on a Φ-member's own surfaces when it is Φ's sole member (the range `J ∈ Φ, J ≠ K'` is then empty; on the shipped-only registry this is exactly `retired`'s own surfaces), and extensionally at any state where no `J ∈ Φ \ {K'}` holds an active tuple filtering a result element. The rewrite is *result-side only* — arguments are never filtered: a filtered source `x` remains a valid default-view `targets_of` argument and answers with its denoted targets minus the filtered ones, so a filtered address vanishes from enumeration results, not from the query surface. Each BH1 type rewrites every *other* type's surfaces, so two BH1 types mutually filter each other's enumerations — each is an "other type" to the other. Both surfaces stay finite and computable — the active-view sets are finite (AD) and each `is_filtered_J` is decided per element. Nothing else is rewritten: the active-view equations D1–D3 keep their values, the membership predicates are untouched, and raw `Observe_K` — both its hist (audit) and oper (active) selectors — never filters. Whether the rewrite extends to behavior-unlocked surfaces — BH3's `sources_to`, BH2's walk through a filtered mid-chain element — is deliberately uncommitted (Open question 1).

Adopted by lifecycle markers that should "vanish" from default queries; the substrate-shipped `retired` is the canonical case.

### BH2 (determinate-walk)

**Applies to:** Binary
**Effect:** Substrate exposes walk predicates over the active view's *denoted graph*: one edge `x → y` per active address-denoting K-tuple whose F denotes `x` and whose G denotes `y` (AD; under `|F| = 1` and Binary's `|G| = 1`, each such tuple contributes exactly one edge), with vertex set the endpoints of those edges — finite, since L-fin bounds the tuples, hence the edges, hence their endpoints. Nothing makes the graph a chain. Binary fixes each *tuple's* `|G| = 1`, not the number of active tuples per source, so a vertex may carry several outgoing edges; and cycles are constructible — emit `x → y` then `y → x`: both gate-pass, neither is I0-equal to the other, and absent retraction both are active. The authorities build the same way: Gregory's link machinery permits cyclic link topologies outright — circular and bidirectional configurations are created and discovered without complaint — and Nelson's supersession is linear by *convention* only: supersession claims are owned links that accumulate, the mechanism admits branching, and adjudicating among competing claims belongs to readers, not the back end. The walk predicates are therefore defined on the general finite digraph, with explicit verdicts where chain language fails — and they go no further than the *determinate* walk the behavior is named for: one-step `succs`, a walk that halts at a branch, no closure or reachability predicate — so a target reachable from `x` only through a branch is beyond all four predicates, by design, not omission.
**Provides:**
- `succs(x) → set of addrs` — the denoted targets of active K-tuples whose F denotes `x` (the argument names a vertex, matched by denotation — AM): one step, no closure.
- `chain(addr) → ordered list of addrs` — the maximal *determinate* walk from `addr`: the longest sequence `addr = x₀, x₁, …, xₙ` with each `xᵢ₊₁` the unique successor (`succs(xᵢ) = {xᵢ₊₁}`) and all elements distinct. The walk stops at `xₙ` when `succs(xₙ) = ∅` (a sink), when `|succs(xₙ)| ≥ 2` (a branch), or when the unique successor already occurs in the sequence (a cycle). The list prepends `addr` itself, which need not be a vertex: an address with no incident edge heads the one-element chain. Termination: each extension appends a unique successor — an edge target, hence a member of the vertex set `V`, which is finite — and `x₁, …, xₙ` are pairwise distinct, so after `n` extensions `t = |V| − n ≥ 0` is a natural-number bound decreasing by one per extension.
- `tip(addr) → addr | ⊥` — `xₙ` when `chain(addr)` stops at a sink (in particular `addr` itself when it has no outgoing edge); `⊥` when the walk stops at a branch or a cycle — the same ⊥ convention BH3's `target_of` uses for the same multiplicity reason.
- `is_in_chain(addr, target) → Bool` — `target ∈ chain(addr)`: membership in the walk's result list — exact denoted vertices, an enumeration-derived test, never a coverage test — so an extension of a chain element does not satisfy it, and tuples with non-denoting endsets never enter the walk it reads (Effect's denoted graph).

Chain-walking reads the active view, never the audit slice: a nullified mid-chain tuple is simply not an edge, so the walk stops earlier (at what is then a sink). BH1 filtering does not rewrite the walk (BH1's rewrite scope). Whether an audit-view chain walk is also needed is left open (Open question 6).

Adopted by chained-replacement relations; the substrate-shipped `supersedes` is the canonical case (S2) — where the verdicts are not decorative: a document carrying two active supersession claims is a branch, `tip = ⊥`.

### BH3 (typed-reverse-lookup)

**Applies to:** Binary
**Effect:** Substrate exposes reverse lookup and a type-keyed forward variant.
**Provides:** `sources_to(target) → set of addrs` — the denoted sources of active K-tuples whose to-coverage contains the target: `⋃ { addrs(F) : (a, F, G) ∈ A_K^Σ ∧ target ∈ coverage(G) }`; the argument is tested by coverage — the enumeration family's one coverage-keyed argument, per AM's rule and for AM's reasons — and the result is denoted addresses (finite, AD). `target_of(source, K) → addr | ⊥` — typed forward lookup: when exactly one active K-tuple's F denotes `source` *and* its G is address-denoting, the denoted target `y` (`addrs(G) = {y}`: one span by Binary, unit-depth by the address-denoting hypothesis); ⊥ in every other case — none (no active tuple's F denotes `source`), several (nothing bounds active tuples per source — BH2's Effect — so functionality in F is an app convention, not a shape guarantee), or a unique tuple whose single G-span is non-unit-depth, so that `addrs(G) = ∅` — a tuple reachable only off-surface (raw `K.λ_sh`; AD's surface encoding is address-denoting by construction), to which AD's omission rule assigns ⊥ rather than a fabricated address. `targets_keyed(source) → map K → addr` — joins `target_of` across every Binary type K registered with BH3; ⊥-valued types are omitted, so the map's keys are exactly those K where `target_of(source, K)` is address-valued.

Adopted by relations where target-side queryability matters operationally — attached auxiliaries, named pointers.

### BH4 (age-staleness)

**Applies to:** any shape, with idem=⊥
**Compatibility, derived.** BH4's three predicates read only the tuple's address, its home-chain index, and the active subset — never G, the arity, or the shape — so a shape clause would be a constraint nothing exercises, and R-C0 imposes none (BH1–BH3 carry shape requirements because their machinery reads the shape's form: `is_filtered` quantifies over Unary's `(a, F, ∅)`, the walk and `target_of` read Binary's `|G| = 1`). The `idem = ⊥` clause is forced by what age must measure. Age is per address, and under `idem = ⊤` an assertion's address is pinned to its first emission: re-emitting an I0-equal tuple is a hit — no step, no fresh chain slot, the incumbent's address returned (I1) — so `age(a)` keeps counting from the original deposit however often the assertion is renewed. `stale(h)` would then classify by first utterance, and `retract_stale` would retract assertions the caller just re-asserted — re-emission being precisely the renewal gesture a lease protocol uses. Under `idem = ⊥` every admitted emit deposits at the frontier (I5): renewal is re-emission, the renewed assertion's age restarts at zero, the unrenewed one ages out.
**Effect:** Substrate provides age-aware queries and batch-retract tooling. The time reference is *ordinal, not temporal*: the state has exactly four components — C, M, L, registry — none of which holds a clock, and R1 forbids the registry from acquiring one. The implementation evidence is emphatic on the same point: Gregory's persistent state records no wall-clock timestamp anywhere — no structure written to disk carries one — and every ordering it supports is structural, so what is recoverable is *which came first*, never *when*. BH4 accordingly measures age by position in the event's home chain (`chain_d` and the frontier `f_d^Σ` — HomedChain and FrontierUnification, ASN-0126).
**Provides:** for an active event tuple at address `a`, `d = home(a)`. Every `a ∈ dom(Σ.L)` lies in its home's contiguous initial segment at a unique chain index — `a = chain_d(j)` for exactly one `j` (L-ContiguousPrefix, ASN-0086, at extended-record states via ASN-0126's B2 and RP-a; uniqueness by strict T1-ascent of `chain_d` under sibling advance) — so `age` is total on active tuples and single-valued:
- `age(a) → ℕ` — `f_d^Σ − 1 − j`: how many deposits homed at `d` postdate the event. The chain interleaves every type homed at `d`, so age counts the home's subsequent link traffic, not K-events alone.
- `stale(h) → set of event-addrs` — `{a : (a, F, G) ∈ A_K^Σ ∧ age(a) > h}` for an ordinal horizon `h ∈ ℕ`; finite and computable (L-fin; age is arithmetic on chain indices). The horizon is *home-relative by design*: `stale` unions over every active K-tuple, but each `age(a)` is denominated in its own home's traffic, and the state holds no cross-home denominator to normalize against — arrival order is recoverable only within a single home's chain, Gregory's store keeping no global counter, log, or cross-document order anywhere. A caller homing K-events across documents therefore gets per-home expiry semantics — the same `h` expires sooner under a busier home, and two events asserted together but homed apart age at different rates — so a lease protocol wanting a single clock must home its K-events in a single document; `retract_stale` inherits the same denomination.
- `retract_stale(d_retr, h)` — for a caller-supplied retracting document `d_retr`: one `Nullify_Binary(·, d_retr, a)` per `a ∈ stale(h)`, the stale set evaluated once at the batch's initial state, `d_retr` held constant across the batch. The retracting document is a parameter, not a substrate default: the wrapper's from-fill makes `d_retr` part of every constituent retraction's identity and attribution (S3), so defaulting it would decide *who* retracts — the caller's decision, not the substrate's. The batch is a *sequence* of `→_sh` steps, one per target, not an atomic operation — interleaving with other operations is permitted. P0 (`d_retr ∈ dom(Σ.M)`) is evaluated once at batch entry — the same state at which the stale set is evaluated — and on failure no constituent is issued. Once it holds there, it persists to every constituent pre-state by domain monotonicity (`dom(Σ.M)` grows along every derivation — Definition Reachability, ASN-0086, carried by RP-b), however the batch interleaves; and each target is an existing link address, so P-tgt's first disjunct holds throughout (L12a per step, ASN-0126's B2 and RP-b) — every constituent call is admitted. A target already retracted — by interleaving, or earlier in the batch — is unproblematic, but by a case split, not by blanket dedup: I0's identity for R pairs the retracting document with the target (S3), so only a re-retraction from the *same* document whose prior retraction tuple is still active is an I1 hit, deduplicating to that tuple. A prior retraction homed in a *different* document is not I0-equal, and a prior retraction tuple itself since nullified is absent from `A_R^Σ` (I2: dedup never consults the audit slice); in both cases the call deposits a fresh retraction tuple — redundant, the target being already nullified (R6b reads `L_R`, where the prior tuple persists), but harmless: an admitted unit-depth wrapper call on an existing link address is contract-conforming, has single-tuple scope, and cannot sterilize (DR). What the constituents add up to is the batch's net postcondition: writing `Σ_fin` for the batch's final state, `(A a : a ∈ stale(h) evaluated at batch entry : a ∈ nullified(Σ_fin))`. Each constituent leaves its target nullified at its own post-state — an admitted miss outright, by the wrapper's miss contract under enforced P-tgt (S3); a hit finds it already so, the matching active retraction tuple supplying R6b's hypotheses at the unchanged state, the target resident by the L12a argument above — and R6a (carried by ASN-0126's B3 and RP-b) preserves `a ∈ nullified(·)` across every later step, remaining constituents and interleaved operations alike, to `Σ_fin`.

An app wanting calendar staleness — seconds rather than slots — must carry timestamps as tuple *content* it supplies itself.

Adopted by bounded leases or short-lived assertions, with the bound denominated in emission traffic rather than seconds.

## Default predicates

Every registered type K, regardless of attached behaviors, exposes three source-side predicates derived from ASN-0086's `Observe_K`. All three are *active-view* definitions; `members` and `targets_of` additionally carry the view selector (View selection, Denotation and views), written into their defining displays below.

**D1 (Members).** `members(K, active) → set of addrs` — the denoted F-addresses of the active K-tuples: `members(K, active) = ⋃ { addrs(F) : (a, F, G) ∈ A_K^Σ }` (unmarked form: View selection). For Unary this is the set of marked addresses; for Binary and Multi it is the set of K-sources. Finite and computable: L-fin bounds the tuples, and each `addrs(F)` is read off finitely many spans (AD).

**D2 (IsK).** `is_K(addr) → Bool` — true iff some `(a, F, G) ∈ A_K^Σ` has `addr ∈ coverage(F)`: the membership regime, total and decidable (AD). It asks whether `addr` falls under the source of an active K-tuple. The two regimes meet where AD says they do, under a hypothesis the *state* carries: call the active K-slice *F-denoting at Σ* when every `(a, F, G) ∈ A_K^Σ` has address-denoting F — which holds at every state a K-surface-emitted derivation (I1a) reaches, the surface depositing canonical encodings only (AD). At F-denoting states, a denoted source's coverage being its subtree (PrefixSpanCoverage), `is_K(addr) ⟺ (E x : x ∈ members(K, active) : x ≼ addr)` — `is_K` holds at the denoted sources and all their extensions, while `members` enumerates the sources themselves. The ⟸ direction holds at every state; ⟹ is what F-denotation buys: an active tuple whose single F-span is non-unit-depth — reachable only off-surface (AD's verdict for nonconforming tuples) — makes `is_K` true throughout its coverage while contributing nothing to `members`. The selector is load-bearing: `is_K` is a membership predicate, never rewritten (BH1's Rewrite scope), so the bridge holds against `members`' `active` reading only — under the `default` reading the right-hand side loses every filtered source and the equivalence fails.

**D3 (TargetsOf).** `targets_of(x, active) → set of addrs` — for a source address `x`, the denoted targets across the active K-tuples whose F denotes `x`: `targets_of(x, active) = ⋃ { addrs(G) : (a, F, G) ∈ A_K^Σ ∧ x ∈ addrs(F) }` (the same for Binary and Multi: Binary's bound is per tuple — BH2's Effect). For Unary, ∅ always. Finite and computable as in D1. The argument is matched by denotation — `x ∈ addrs(F)`, per AM — and I0's assertion-as-subtree doctrine is honored one composition up, by the same bridge D2 builds for `is_K`. A tuple whose F denotes `t` asserts for every extension `t.x` — `is_K(t.x) = ⊤` (D2) — yet `targets_of(t.x, active) = ∅` when no tuple's F denotes `t.x` itself; the two answers live at different layers, not in contradiction. The assertion-level forward query is the derived composition

`targets_under(addr) = ⋃ { targets_of(x, active) : x ∈ members(K, active) ∧ x ≼ addr }`

— finite and computable (D1's bounds, prefix tests componentwise), an app-side composition rather than a fourth shipped predicate. The selectors are part of the recipe: the unmarked composition (View selection) is a *different* query — both constituents BH1-filtered — that can drop sources and targets the active view holds. `targets_under` commits to the active view; at states whose active K-slice is F-denoting (D2's hypothesis) it *equals* the coverage-keyed alternative `⋃ { addrs(G) : (a, F, G) ∈ A_K^Σ ∧ addr ∈ coverage(F) }`, since for an address-denoting F `addr ∈ coverage(F) ⟺ (E x : x ∈ addrs(F) : x ≼ addr)` (PrefixSpanCoverage). Off the hypothesis the two diverge — the coverage-keyed union additionally collects `addrs(G)` from active tuples whose non-denoting F covers `addr`, sources no `members` listing reaches — and the default-view composition forfeits the equality in any case whenever some BH1 type's active tuple filters a contributing source or target. The denotation-keyed form is the primitive because it is the finer datum: `targets_under` is assembled from `targets_of` by union over the D2 bridge, while `targets_of` is not recoverable from `targets_under` — the union forgets which source contributed which targets. D2 answers at the assertion level, D3 at the vertex level, and `targets_under` answers D2's question with D3's data.

**D4 (ReverseAccessIsBehavioral).** Target-side queries — "which sources point at a given target" — are not in the default set. They require the BH3 (typed-reverse-lookup) behavior, which provides `sources_to(target)`. The default trio reads only forward: source membership and source-to-target projection. Reverse access is a behavior-conditional capability, deliberately opt-in: a type that does not need reverse access carries no substrate commitment to its existence.

D1–D3 are well-defined on every reachable state by ASN-0086's active-subset definition, AD's finiteness, and R1 ensuring K's registration record is consulted identically at every state.

## Standard registrations

Does any type ship pre-registered, or is each substrate's `Σ_init.registry` composed entirely of app-declared entries? Three coverage classes ship in `Σ_init.registry` for every substrate, supplied as part of substrate initialization rather than app registration. Every other type is app-registered; the substrate ships only the lifecycle, supersession, and retraction universals.

Which three classes is a *substrate parameter*, in the manner of the subspace identifiers `s_C` and `s_L` (ASN-0043) and of ASN-0086's designated retraction class `[R]`: the substrate fixes three representative endsets `K_ret, K_sup, K_R ∈ T_admissible` — concretely, say, one unit-depth span each at three reserved *type addresses*. (The implementation precedent is exact: Gregory's store reserves conventional tumbler addresses in a bootstrap document as a type registry — jump, quote, and footnote types each sit at a fixed address, with subtyping encoded by tumbler containment — and a link's type is a reference into that reserved region.) The names `retired`, `supersedes`, `R` below denote the coverage classes `[K_ret]`, `[K_sup]`, `[K_R]` of the designated representatives, and the operation surface exposes the representatives under exactly these names — an emit under the name uses the stored representative, and any `~`-equal endset names the same class (the slice subscript is read modulo `~`, ASN-0086).

**R-C1 (DesignationNonCollision).** The three designated classes are pairwise non-`~`-equivalent: `[K_ret] ≠ [K_sup]`, `[K_sup] ≠ [K_R]`, `[K_R] ≠ [K_ret]`. The three entries being mandatory, a colliding designation would violate C0's key uniqueness and no `Σ_init` — no substrate at all — could be constructed (enforcement: C0, R-VAL): a parameterization whose representatives collide fails construction outright, exactly as a colliding app declaration does (the multi-app merge protocol remains Open question 8).

**S1 (Retired).** `retired` (the designated class `[K_ret]`) — Unary, idem=⊤, behaviors={BH1}. Marks an address as lifecycle-retired: the default view on every other type excludes it from enumeration results, with the exclusion's coverage scope per BH1's Effect and Rewrite scope. Active subsets are untouched — nothing is nullified, and the marked content remains reachable under the `active` selector (View selection).

**S2 (Supersedes).** `supersedes` (the designated class `[K_sup]`) — Binary, idem=⊤, behaviors={BH2}. Records that one address supersedes another, under a slot convention the registration itself must pin — L7 (ASN-0043) places directional interpretation with the type, not the structure, so a shipped registration that left it open would let two conforming implementations walk opposite directions. The convention: a tuple asserts that the address its F denotes *is superseded by* the address its G denotes — edges run old → new. `succs` thus steps from a version to its claimed replacements, and `tip()` resolves to the current head when the active supersession edges from the queried address form a determinate, acyclic walk, and to ⊥ at a branch or a cycle (BH2's verdicts). The name names the relation the class records, not a left-to-right reading of the slots. The supersession link is the canonical whole-document metalink of Nelson's design (the pattern ASN-0126's attribution analysis grounds in LM 4/52–4/53) — whose arrow runs the other way: LM's diagram draws supersession from the new version to the old, the from-side carrying the superseding document. The registration stores the converse deliberately. Nelson's canonical query enters at the old end — a front end checks for supersession before presenting a document, in his protocol by searching the *to*-sides of links aimed at the document in hand, reading the metalink against its arrow under direction-symmetric search machinery — while BH2's walk is forward-only, F → G, its argument a denoted *source* (AM). Orienting edges old → new aligns the determinate walk with the canonical query: from the version in hand, forward to the current head. And the practicality that fixes LM's direction — the link ships with the new document, the old one being beyond further editing — has no analog here: a tuple's home is its emitter's `d`, independent of either endset (L2, ASN-0043), so the old address sits in F without anyone touching the old document.

**S3 (Retraction).** `R` (the designated class `[K_R]`, ASN-0086's RetractionType) — Binary, idem=⊤, behaviors=∅. The retraction relation; ASN-0126 (Retraction as an attributed Binary) establishes the Binary registration and specifies the live retraction operation as a full contract: the unit-depth wrapper `Nullify_Binary(Σ, d_retr, a) ≡ Emit_R(Σ, d_retr, {r}, {(a, δ(1, #a))})` with canonical from-fill `r = (d_retr, δ(1, #d_retr))`, preconditions P0 (`d_retr ∈ dom(Σ.M)`) and P-reg ([R] registered Binary), and postconditions: unconditional coverage nullification, target-nullification iff residence, single-tuple scope iff P-tgt, and persistence (R6a/R6c carried across by ASN-0126's bridge lemmas B2 and B3, and onward to this note's extended-record states by RP-b). Where ASN-0126 frames R as app-registered, this note ships it — with R in `Σ_init.registry`, retraction is uniformly available without per-app declaration, and the contract's P-reg precondition is discharged globally, on every substrate. Binary is a *choice* among the four [R] configurations ASN-0126 sweeps: unregistered [R] gives an audit-only substrate where no `Emit_R` has a `→_sh` image; Unary [R] forces `G = ∅`, emptying every retraction's to-coverage — retraction-inert; and Multi [R] expresses retraction just as well — the wrapper's `|F| = 1, |G| = 1` emission passes a Multi gate — but admits multi-span to-endsets through the gate on raw deposits. Shipping Binary selects the configuration where retraction is expressible *and* the gate itself confines every R-tuple, wrapper-routed or bypassed, to a single-span to-endset — the only form a gate-passed R-tuple can carry (ASN-0126's P6) and the form RangeSterilization's analysis reads — accepting that the wp's C2/C3 conjuncts are live (ASN-0126, Weakest precondition).

The shipped registration carries an operation-surface policy. ASN-0126's contract deliberately puts **no** precondition on the target — P-tgt "is not assumed, it conditions the postconditions": single-tuple scope holds *iff* P-tgt held at the pre-state, and the gate, being static, cannot check the residence disjunct. The operational layer is not so constrained: operations already read state (I1's dedup reads `A_K^Σ`). This note therefore exposes `Nullify_Binary` as the **only** retraction entry point — the exposed `Emit_K` rejects every R-class call by its uniform `K ≁ R` precondition (I6) — and *promotes P-tgt from postcondition hypothesis to enforced precondition*: `a ∈ A_rel^Σ` or `a = a_emit(Σ, d_retr)`. Enforced means *rejecting*: a call failing P-tgt (or P0, or P-reg) takes no `→_sh` step — the state is unchanged and no address is returned. The alternative — admit the failing call under ASN-0126's bare contract — would let a single wrapper call sterilize a document outright: any tumbler is T12-admissible as a unit-depth to-span target (Nullify_Binary, ASN-0126), so an admitted ghost-target call at `a = d.0.s_L` deposits to-coverage `subtree(d.0.s_L)`, which contains every chain slot `d.0.s_L.m` — every future `a_emit(·, d)` — and every subsequent emission homed at `d` is born nullified (Corollary RangeSterilization, ASN-0126): total sterilization of `d`, achieved *through the wrapper*. The containment claims of this section are true only under rejection, which makes sterilization (Corollary RangeSterilization) unreachable through substrate-mediated retraction. (Since `nullified` reads only `L_R` — the one coverage class R occupies — no app-registered type can sterilize either: containment is complete.)

The policy buys back what ASN-0126's gated layer lost, at every state a surface-disciplined derivation reaches (SD).

**DR (DisciplineRestoration — proof and wrapper wp).** DR's statement (Idem operational semantics). The wp the vacuity buys the wrapper: *for single-tuple scope — the postcondition of ASN-0086's wp Case 1 — the weakest precondition at this surface is the operation's own:*

`wp(Nullify_Binary(Σ, d_retr, a), {t : a ≼ t} ∩ A_rel^{Σ'} = {a}) ≡ P0 ∧ P-reg ∧ P-tgt` — at every state a surface-disciplined derivation reaches (SD)

The equivalence is read under I6's attainability convention, and its two halves hold on different domains — the qualifier scopes sufficiency alone. *Necessity, at every reachable state.* DR's postcondition, unlike I6's POST, mentions no returned address, so rejection does not falsify it for free: a call failing P0 alone against a resident target `a ∈ A_rel^Σ` is rejected with `Σ' = Σ`, and the postcondition *holds* at the unchanged state — ⊇ by residence and reflexivity of ≼, ⊆ by R0a's antichain, both sides being link addresses — so under a bare guarantee reading P0 would not be necessary and the displayed equivalence would be false. Under the convention, necessity holds per precondition, reading the discipline nowhere: a call failing P0 or P-reg is rejected — no step — so the wp is false by the convention alone; a call failing P-tgt (with P0 ∧ P-reg, so `a_emit(Σ, d_retr)` is defined) is likewise rejected, and there the postcondition is genuinely false at the unchanged state — ¬P-tgt gives `a ∉ A_rel^Σ = A_rel^{Σ'}`, so the intersection omits `a` and cannot equal `{a}` — necessity under either reading. *Sufficiency, on the disciplined domain.* The derivation below, completed per branch after the contract.

Fix a surface-disciplined derivation. Every R-tuple present at a state it reaches entered at some `L_R`-growing step along it, which SD classifies as a `Nullify_Binary` invocation — its deposit branch, a hit growing nothing — so the tuple's to-coverage is a subtree `{t : a ≼ t}` at a target `a` that was P-tgt-valid at that step's pre-state: the wrapper, rejecting P-tgt failures without a step, deposits no other (S3). We must show no later emit's fresh address `f` falls inside it — `¬(a ≼ f)`. Both P-tgt branches place `a` in the link domain no later than the depositing step's own post-state: a pre-existing target is in the pre-state's `dom(Σ.L)` already, and a self-emit target is the deposit's own emitter. By domain monotonicity (L12a, carried per step by ASN-0126's B2 and across extended-record derivations by RP-b) `a` remains a link address at every later state of the derivation. *Distinctness:* at a later emit's pre-state Θ — any state of the derivation where the retraction tuple already sits in `L_R^Θ` — the fresh address satisfies `f = a_emit(Θ, d) ∉ dom(Θ.L)` while `a ∈ dom(Θ.L)`, so `f ≠ a`. *Antichain:* the emit's `K.λ_sh` step fires regardless of C3 — C3 governs only whether the deposit lands active — so its post-state Θ' is reachable (RP-c), with `f ∈ dom(Θ'.L)` by the deposit and `a ∈ dom(Θ'.L)` by monotonicity. Instantiating R0a (FlatLinkDomain, ASN-0086, via ASN-0126's B2 and RP-a) at Θ': `dom(Θ'.L)` is a prefix antichain, so `a ≼ f` would force `a = f`, contradicting distinctness. Hence `f ∉ {t : a ≼ t}`, and no policy-emitted retraction's coverage ever contains a later fresh address — C3's existential is empty over `L_R^Θ` at every state Θ a surface-disciplined derivation reaches.

For `Nullify_Binary` itself the conclusion must be split by branch: R ships `idem = ⊤` (S3) and `Nullify_Binary ≡ Emit_R`, so the wrapper inherits I1's de-duplication, and an admitted call may take no step at all.

*Preconditions — uniform.* P0 (`d_retr ∈ dom(Σ.M)`), P-reg ([R] registered Binary — discharged globally by the shipped registration), P-tgt (`a ∈ A_rel^Σ ∨ a = a_emit(Σ, d_retr)`), checked on every call, hits included, P0 first — P-tgt's self-emit disjunct mentions `a_emit(Σ, d_retr)`, total only under P0. A call failing any of the three is rejected: no step, no address (S3). Uniform P0, against I1's branch-local home validation for a general `Emit_K`, is the same validate-where-read principle: the wrapper's from-fill puts `subtree(d_retr)` into its I0 identity, so `d_retr` is read on both branches.

*Branch condition.* The admitted call is a *hit* iff some `(b', F', G') ∈ A_R^Σ` has `coverage(F') = subtree(d_retr)` and `coverage(G') = subtree(a)` — I0 instantiated at the wrapper's unit-depth endsets by PrefixSpanCoverage — and a *miss* otherwise.

*Miss.* The wrapper takes the `K.λ_sh` step — admitted at extended-record states by RP-c — and ASN-0126's contract holds verbatim: fresh emitter `b = a_emit(Σ, d_retr)`, `home(b) = d_retr`, deposit `({r}, {(a, δ(1, #a))}, R)`, `A_rel^{Σ'} = A_rel^Σ ∪ {b}`; and, P-tgt being enforced, the iff-P-tgt postconditions hold outright: `a ∈ nullified(Σ')`, coverage nullification, single-tuple scope `{t : a ≼ t} ∩ A_rel^{Σ'} = {a}`, and persistence (RP-b).

*Hit.* **No step**: `Σ' = Σ`, every Effect clause is forfeited — no fresh emitter, `dom(Σ'.L) = dom(Σ.L)`, `A_rel^{Σ'} = A_rel^Σ` — and the wrapper returns the existing tuple's address (unique by I1a — a surface-disciplined derivation is [R]-surface-emitted, SD). The remaining guarantees are *re-established at* `Σ' = Σ` from the pre-existing tuple, not concluded from a step:

- *Residence.* The self-emit disjunct cannot hit along a surface-disciplined derivation: the matching to-coverage `subtree(a)` determines its root uniquely (mutual `≼` forces equality), that root was the matching tuple's own P-tgt-valid target at its emit — already resident, or the deposit's own emitter — hence in the link domain ever since (L12a per step, ASN-0126's B2 and RP-b), while the self-emit candidate `a_emit(Σ, d_retr)` is fresh, `∉ dom(Σ.L)`. A hit is therefore admitted by the first disjunct: `a ∈ A_rel^Σ`.
- *Nullification.* The matching tuple sits in `A_R^Σ ⊆ L_R^Σ` with `a ∈ coverage(G')` (reflexivity of `≼`), and `a ∈ A_rel^Σ` by residence — exactly the three hypotheses of R6b (SingleDepthRetraction, ASN-0086, via ASN-0126's B2 and RP-a), so `a ∈ nullified(Σ)`; every resident `t` with `a ≼ t` satisfies them likewise — coverage nullification holds at Σ.
- *Single-tuple scope.* `{t : a ≼ t} ∩ A_rel^{Σ'} = {a}`: ⊇ is residence with `a ≼ a`; for ⊆, a resident `t` with `a ≼ t` has `a, t ∈ dom(Σ.L)`, and the antichain R0a (FlatLinkDomain, ASN-0086, via ASN-0126's B2 and RP-a) forces `t = a`.
- *Persistence.* `a ∈ nullified(Σ)` persists at every `→_sh*`-successor, and a nullified tuple stays out of every active subset there (R6a/R6c by ASN-0126's B3 and RP-b).

Both branches thus deliver single-tuple scope at the call's post-state — by the step's contract on a miss, holding at every reachable state, by the pre-existing tuple on a hit, the one branch that consumes the discipline (its Residence bullet) — which supplies the sufficiency half of the displayed wp on its stated domain; necessity was discharged at the display at every reachable state, so the equivalence is complete. A derivation off the discipline (SD) leaves DR's scope at its first non-wrapper `L_R`-growing step, and the two forms such a step's tuple can take defeat DR by different routes. A *range-G* tuple defeats C3: the chain slots its to-coverage includes are sterilized — every deposit landing there is born nullified — and there I2's sterilization caveat is the operative bound. A *unit-depth* bypass tuple crafted in the wrapper's own endset form — from-fill `(d_retr, δ(1, #d_retr))`, to-span at a chain address still ahead of `d_retr`'s frontier, a ghost at deposit time — defeats the hit branch instead: once the frontier reaches the target, a legal self-emit call `Nullify_Binary(Σ, d_retr, a)` I0-matches the bypass tuple, dedups to it, and takes no step — `a` never enters the link domain, so `{t : a ≼ t} ∩ A_rel^{Σ'} = ∅ ≠ {a}` and nothing is nullified: the caller's retraction silently no-ops. That is precisely the case the hit branch's Residence bullet rules out *under* the discipline; off it, the bullet's L12a argument has no purchase on a tuple whose target was a ghost at its deposit — and the silent no-op is the witness that the display's SD qualifier cannot be dropped: a reachable state at which P0 ∧ P-reg ∧ P-tgt holds and the postcondition fails.

No behavior is attached to R: retraction's effect on `A_K^Σ` is built into ASN-0086's active-subset semantics rather than surfaced as a derived predicate. The wrapper's from-fill has two consequences. First, attribution is observable without BH3: `Observe_R` matches the from-fill at whole-document granularity — ASN-0126 scopes this match to *wrapper-routed* retractions (a gated `Emit_R` whose one from-span lies elsewhere escapes the under-`d_retr` pattern), and under this note's wrapper-only surface (the `K ≁ R` precondition, I6) that qualifier saturates at every state a surface-disciplined derivation reaches (SD): there every retraction is wrapper-routed, so the convention — F answers *who retracts*, G carries *what is retracted* — holds throughout the disciplined domain. It is no wider: raw R-class `K.λ_sh` deposits remain `→_sh`-reachable, and such a tuple's from-span need not name its emitter's document. Second, idem identity degenerates in F: the from-fill is per-document, so by I0 two retractions are the same iff they share the retracting document and a coverage-equal target — re-retracting the same target from the same document dedups to the existing tuple, which is the desired idempotence of retraction.

**The operation set.** `{Emit_K, Observe_K, Nullify_Binary}`: `Emit_K` is normatively fixed by the exposed signature and I6 — the idem-⊤ contract and its idem-⊥ corollary, one consolidated wp per flag value (Idem operational semantics) — `Nullify_Binary` by S3 above, and `Observe_K` is ASN-0086's read unchanged, with the behavior and default predicates layered on it. One behavior-provided surface writes rather than reads: BH4's `retract_stale` is batch tooling over the third primitive — a sequence of `Nullify_Binary` invocations, one per stale target (BH4) — so every `→_sh` step it contributes is a wrapper step, extending surface-disciplined derivations (SD) and introducing no fourth primitive.

## An abstract registry example

The example below uses generic type names — `marker`, `aux`, `fanout`, `event` — to illustrate behaviors against shapes the substrate-shipped registrations don't exhaust. Throughout, tuples are written by their denoted addresses (AD): `F = a_src` abbreviates the singleton address-denoting endset `{(a_src, δ(1, #a_src))}`, and `G = [a_t1, a_t2, a_t3]` the endset holding one canonical span per listed address.

Consider a registry composed of the three substrate-shipped entries plus four illustrative app entries, each a full registration record `(shape, idem, behaviors)`:

| Name | Shape | idem | Behaviors | Source |
|------|-------|------|-----------|--------|
| `retired` | Unary | ⊤ | read-filter | substrate |
| `supersedes` | Binary | ⊤ | determinate-walk | substrate |
| `R` | Binary | ⊤ | — | substrate |
| `marker` | Unary | ⊤ | — | illustrative |
| `aux` | Binary | ⊤ | typed-reverse-lookup | illustrative |
| `fanout` | Multi | ⊤ | — | illustrative |
| `event` | Multi | ⊥ | age-staleness | illustrative |

The four illustrative entries cover the shape-and-behavior cells the substrate-shipped three leave unexhausted: Unary without read-filter (`marker`), Binary with typed-reverse-lookup (`aux`), Multi with idem=⊤ (`fanout`), and Multi with idem=⊥ + age-staleness (`event`).

**Emit under idem=⊤ Multi.** An emit of `fanout` with `F = a_src`, `G = [a_t1, a_t2, a_t3]` runs the gate: K registered (✓), Sh-conf with |F|=1 (✓), `K.λ_sh` admits. The active-subset check finds no existing coverage-equal tuple, so the emit produces a fresh address. A repeat emit with the same `(F, G)` — or one whose G additionally lists an extension of an already-listed target, a different address set with identical coverage (`subtree(a_t1.x) ⊆ subtree(a_t1)`, PrefixSpanCoverage; I0 reads coverage) — finds the now-active tuple, returns its address, takes no step, and ignores its `d` argument (I1's hit branch). In the second case the call's presentation is suppressed (I1's hit clause): `a_t1.x` is never stored, so enumeration thereafter reflects the incumbent's denoted set, `targets_of(a_src) = {a_t1, a_t2, a_t3}` with `a_t1.x` absent, while the matching surfaces still see it: an `Observe` to-pattern at `a_t1.x` matches the incumbent tuple, whose `coverage(G)` contains it.

**Emit under idem=⊥ Multi with age-staleness.** An emit of `event` with `F = a_actor`, `G = [a_obj]` produces a fresh address regardless of any existing event — the frontier slot of its home chain, whose index is the event's ordinal birthmark; nothing is recorded beyond the deposit itself, the state having no clock. `age(event_addr)` returns how many later deposits the home has accumulated. A later `retract_stale(d_app, 600)` — `d_app` the caller's retracting document, named because the from-fill attributes every constituent retraction to it (S3) — issues one `Nullify_Binary(·, d_app, a)` per active event `a` whose age exceeds 600: a sequence of wrapper steps, not an atomic operation, each P0-valid (`d_app ∈ dom(Σ.M)`, constant across the batch), unit-depth, and P-tgt-valid (the target is an existing link address), so the batch cannot sterilize.

**Read-filter on Unary lifecycle.** Emit `marker` on `a_x`, then emit `retired` on the same `a_x`. `is_retired(a_x)` returns true; the unmarked call `members(marker)` reads the default view and excludes `a_x` (BH1's rewrite). Only the default-view visibility is filtered: the original `marker` tuple is still in `A_{marker}^Σ` — nothing nullified it — and `members(marker, active)` returns it (View selection, Denotation and views).

**Typed-reverse-lookup on Binary.** Emit `aux` with `F = a_p`, `G = [a_aux1]`. `target_of(a_p, aux)` returns `a_aux1`. A separate emit of a different Binary type — say a hypothetical second `aux2`, registered Binary with typed-reverse-lookup attached (`target_of` is BH3-unlocked, and `targets_keyed` joins only the BH3-registered Binary types) — at `F = a_p` with `G = [a_aux2]` gives `target_of(a_p, aux2) = a_aux2`, and `targets_keyed(a_p)` returns `{aux: a_aux1, aux2: a_aux2}`.

**Determinate walk on `supersedes`.** Three document addresses `a_v1, a_v2, a_v3`. Emit `supersedes` with `F = a_v1`, `G = a_v2` — `a_v1` is superseded by `a_v2` (S2's slot convention) — then with `F = a_v2`, `G = a_v3`. The denoted graph holds the edges `a_v1 → a_v2 → a_v3`, old to new. Queried from the oldest version, `chain(a_v1) = [a_v1, a_v2, a_v3]` and `tip(a_v1) = a_v3`: the walk runs from the version in hand to the current head — Nelson's front-end question, answered by the forward walk. Queried from the head, `succs(a_v3) = ∅`, so `tip(a_v3) = a_v3`: an address with no active supersession claim is its own tip. A competing claim — a second active tuple with `F = a_v2`, `G = a_v3'` — makes `a_v2` a branch: `succs(a_v2) = {a_v3, a_v3'}`, and `tip(a_v1) = tip(a_v2) = ⊥` (BH2's verdicts). The substrate reports the multiplicity; adjudicating among competing claims belongs to readers (BH2's Effect).

**Born-nullified case (I3).** Suppose a pre-existing R-tuple in the audit slice `L_R^Σ` has to-endset covering some address `a*` that `K.λ_sh` is about to allocate as the fresh address of a `fanout` emit with `F = a_src` — exactly the failure of wp conjunct C3, whose existential ranges over `L_R^Σ` (I3). The covering tuple's own status is immaterial: `nullified` consults the audit slice, never the active subset, so a covering retraction itself since nullified still bars the deposit (R6b, ASN-0086). The gate admits (the emit is shape-conformant under `fanout`'s Multi registration), and the new tuple is deposited at `a*` in `L_{fanout}^{Σ'}`; with `a* ∈ nullified(Σ')`, it is absent from `A_{fanout}^{Σ'}` — `A_K` is `L_K` minus the nullified addresses (ASN-0086's active-subset semantics) — born nullified. The tuple contributes nothing to any default predicate query on `fanout`: it does not enlarge `members(fanout)`, does not surface in `targets_of`, and a later idem=⊤ emit with the same `(F, G)` will not see it (per I2). Note such a covering R-tuple cannot arise through `Nullify_Binary` (DR); the case is reachable only along a derivation that is not surface-disciplined (SD) — pre-policy history or a bypassed operation surface — and the gate-level question this leaves is Open question 3.

## What this note doesn't cover

- **Predicate composition.** Composition rules over the atomic default predicates and the behavior-unlocked predicates — connectives, nesting, and the algebra by which compound queries are assembled from atomic ones, including closure of the predicate set two behaviors on one type jointly unlock (e.g., determinate-walk and typed-reverse-lookup on the same Binary, otherwise orthogonal). A separate successor (ASN-0126 Open Question 5).
- **Reachability over the denoted graph.** BH2 ships one-step `succs` and the determinate walk, no closure: `reach(x, y)` over the denoted digraph would be finite and decidable by the same L-fin bounds, but both authorities place multi-hop traversal outside the system — Gregory's machinery answers every link query in a single pass (set intersection over endsets; chains are walked by the client issuing repeated single-step queries), and Nelson leaves following a supersession chain to the reader. An app computes closure from `succs`; promoting `reach` to a substrate obligation would redraw that line and waits for a forcing case.
- **Range-valued endsets.** The operation surface encodes address sets as canonical unit-depth spans (AD); the narrowing is this surface's, not the substrate's. Gregory's link-creation surface is range-valued end to end — every endpoint a span set whose widths are semantically live — and endsets whose spans carry *extent* remain expressible at the substrate (L4's generality is untouched; raw `K.λ_sh`), but no predicate this note ships enumerates them. A range-aware predicate layer is deferred.
- **Extensions beyond F=1 and N=3.** Multi-span sources and richer arity are ASN-0126 Open Question 6's territory — a supplemental note loosening the constraints, or a parallel framework; this note inherits the F=1, N=3 envelope unchanged.
- **Cascade-with and single-source-restriction.** Two behaviors considered but not exercised by any registration this note commits on. Deferred until a forcing function appears.

## Open questions

1. **BH1 × BH2 and BH1 × BH3 interaction on filtered elements.** BH1's committed rewrite scope is `members` and `targets_of` only, so the behavior-unlocked surfaces are untouched as specified — but should they be? When an address carries an active `read-filter` (BH1) tuple and is also a target of some Binary type with `typed-reverse-lookup` (BH3), what does `sources_to(filtered_addr)` return — the empty set (BH1 dominates) or the actual sources (BH3 dominates)? And when a mid-chain element of a `supersedes` chain carries an active `retired` mark, should a default-view `chain()`/`tip()` pass through it (the active-view walk, as currently specified), stop, or skip? `retired` and `supersedes` both ship, so the BH1 × BH2 case is the canonical lifecycle scenario, not a corner. Either dominance choice is defensible; this note pins only the active-view semantics.

2. **Cross-type composite queries.** When a query spans types with different idem values (e.g., counting active tuples of one idem=⊤ Multi type *and* one idem=⊥ Multi type matching some predicate), what does the composite count commit on — set-semantics on each type's active subset and sum the cardinalities? The bag-vs-set question splits cleanly per type, but composite queries inherit both.

3. **Gate-level reinforcement of sterilization containment.** S3 contains sterilization at the operation surface (`Nullify_Binary`-only, P-tgt rejecting — the error semantics are fixed in S3, not open here). Should the gate also be refined — making non-unit G non-conformant for R specifically — as defense in depth, at the cost of a type-sensitive gate clause ASN-0126 deliberately avoided?

4. **Attribution of substrate-shipped types.** The three standard registrations (`retired`, `supersedes`, `R`) ship in `Σ_init.registry` without an "originating app." Whether `provenance.import` (or similar) should be emitted for them at Σ_init to give them an explicit provenance, or whether substrate-shipped types are inherently un-attributable, is unspecified.

5. **Behavior catalog growth.** Candidates considered but excluded from the four: conflict-detection (idem=⊤ violation surfacing), materialization (view caching as a substrate concern), behavior versioning (when a behavior's semantics evolve). Each could ship in a supplemental note when a forcing use case appears.

6. **Audit-view chain walking.** BH2's chain predicates read the active subset, so a nullified mid-chain tuple breaks the chain. Whether an audit-view variant of `chain()`/`tip()` — walking `L_K^Σ` to recover historical chains — is a substrate obligation or an app-side reconstruction is left open.

7. **Registry evolution.** Registration remains construction-time-only: an app that needs a new type after `Σ_init` has no path here, and P1/R1 are built on that immutability. Migration to a successor registry, substrate rebuild, or a versioned-registry relaxation of P1 (per-epoch stability) are all coherent directions; each is new machinery this note deliberately does not open.

8. **Multi-app registry composition.** This note's `Σ_init.registry` is the three substrate-shipped entries plus app declarations, but nothing governs how *several* apps sharing one substrate merge their declarations into the single registry — or what resolves a coverage-class collision, between two apps or between an app and a substrate-shipped key. C0's key uniqueness states the constraint; the construction protocol that achieves it is unspecified.

9. **Retraction-attribution query semantics.** With every retraction wrapper-routed, the from-fill makes "all retractions by document `d`" expressible as an `Observe_R` from-pattern (S3). What query language this supports — pattern algebra over retraction attributions, composition with the default predicates, interaction with BH1 filtering of the retracting document itself — is unaddressed; S3 fixes the convention, not the queries over it.
